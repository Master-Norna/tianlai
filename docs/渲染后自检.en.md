# Post-render self-check

The post-render self-check examines the PCM WAV that has actually been written
to disk and will be delivered to the user, rather than an intermediate floating-
point buffer. It is a strict baseline quality gate, but it does not decide
whether music sounds good and never normalizes, filters, repairs phase, trims a
tail, edits a score, or otherwise changes audio automatically.

## Two kinds of conclusion

The report keeps two conclusions separate:

- **Hard contracts** cover damage or contradictions that can be demonstrated
  objectively: a WAV that cannot be decoded completely, a wrong container or
  bit depth, a sample rate/channel/frame count that conflicts with the
  performance plan, a modified report or audio Hash, or exact digital silence
  when the plan explicitly contains positive-velocity sounding events. Such an
  error prevents an incomplete generation from being published and has no
  generic `force` bypass.
- **Creator-review findings** identify technically risky conditions that may
  still be intentional: insufficient true-peak headroom, DC, mono-fold
  cancellation, extreme channel imbalance, near silence, a full-scale plateau,
  or a possibly truncated tail. They never change audio and never reject a work
  merely for violating a universalized aesthetic preference.

The existing pre-quantization gates remain authoritative for NaN, Infinity, and
samples outside `[-1, 1]`. A check of already quantized PCM cannot reconstruct
those facts reliably and does not replace the existing gates.

## Artifacts and bindings

Ensemble, project-candidate, and MCP renders always produce:

```text
合奏.wav
渲染后自检.json
渲染回执.json
```

A newly written v3 render receipt binds the self-check report by SHA-256. The
report in turn binds the exact WAV, canonical performance plan, audio format,
measurement-algorithm version, and decision-policy version. The receipt remains
the last artifact published, so a staging analysis, validation, or publication
failure cannot present half of a new generation as a complete candidate. Old
v2 receipts remain verifiable under their original contract and are not
rewritten to pretend that a historical self-check existed.

A standalone `tianlai render` writes this adjacent report:

```text
<filename>.wav.渲染后自检.json
```

It is transactionally published with the WAV, license JSON, and human-readable
attribution notice; the WAV remains the final commit marker.

This self-check covers one render's audio contract. To revalidate a complete
project candidate after copying or archiving it, use `candidate-verify`; it
also checks the candidate manifest, receipt, every bound artifact, and the
closed-directory boundary, but likewise proves neither authorship nor
provenance.

## Measurements

The report records at least:

- WAV/PCM24/stereo structure, sample rate, frames, byte size, and SHA-256;
- per-channel and overall sample peak, RMS, crest factor, DC, exact-zero, and
  full-scale statistics;
- L/R correlation, Mid/Side energy, mono-fold loss, and channel balance;
- head/tail windows, the final sample, final-frame jump, longest exact-zero run,
  and peak position;
- ITU-R BS.1770-5 / EBU Mode Integrated LUFS, maximum Momentary loudness, and
  maximum Short-term loudness;
- Annex 2 band-limited oversampled True Peak; and
- EBU Tech 3342 Loudness Range, explicitly marked unstable and descriptive for
  material shorter than 60 seconds.

The machine-readable contract is
[`post-render-check.schema.json`](../schemas/post-render-check.schema.json).

Analysis streams with bounded memory instead of copying a long work wholly into
RAM. Reports omit timestamps, duration-to-compute, absolute paths, and machine
identity so identical inputs remain auditable across output directories and
warm or cold caches.
Version 1 claims standards compliance for loudness and True Peak only at the
project's formal default of `48 kHz`. Other supported sample rates still receive
all structure, sample-domain, DC, stereo, and boundary checks; standards metrics
are explicitly `unavailable` instead of presenting an unvalidated approximation
as a BS.1770 result.

## Interpreting loudness and true peak

BS.1770 specifies loudness and True Peak measurement algorithms, not one
mandatory loudness target for every kind of music. EBU R 128's `-23 LUFS`
target is for broadcast programme normalization and must not reject every work
outside that neighbourhood. LRA likewise has no universal brick-wall maximum
across genres.

The report raises a high-confidence review finding when True Peak is above the
commonly used `-1 dBTP` distribution-headroom line; reconstructed peaks above
`0 dBTP` carry a more direct overload risk. Later filtering, encoding, or
playback may raise peaks further, but version 1 does not invalidate the
creator's work on that basis. A formal delivery should additionally apply the
target platform's own loudness and peak specification.

## Human listening remains necessary

Machines can reliably find damage, identity mismatches, and review candidates;
they cannot reliably decide whether a timbre is refined, dynamics are moving, a
tail fits the phrase, or a transient is a pop rather than an intentional hit.
Click, dropout, spectral, and tail findings especially require event timing,
stems, and actual listening. The self-check supplies locatable evidence instead
of presenting a subjective conclusion as a mathematical law.
