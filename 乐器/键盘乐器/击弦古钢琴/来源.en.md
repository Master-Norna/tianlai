## Current selection: SIMPK 03 Clavichord

- Official project page: <https://www.simpk.de/museum/besuch/projekte/klaviatur-tastatur-interface/samples.html>
- Official archive: <https://topographie.simpk.de/kti/SIMPK_03_Clavichord.zip>
- Publisher and required attribution: Staatliches Institut für Musikforschung (SIMPK)
- Physical instrument: Johann Gottlob Horn clavichord, Dresden, circa 1793
- Upstream version: 1.0; server archive timestamp `2022-10-21`
- License: CC BY 4.0; the original license text is `LICENSE` inside the archive
- Local resource root: `音源/SIMPK_03_Clavichord`

SIMPK's official description of the complete museum series says that each
pitch was recorded individually, with multiple velocities and articulations
where feasible. It also explicitly notes that centuries-old instruments can
have mechanical noise and local tuning deviations. That series-level
description does not replace verification of this particular archive.

The archive preset is arranged as
`63 keys × 3 velocity zones × 2 RR × 2 timbres = 756` WAV paths. SHA-256 over
fully decoded PCM finds only 252 unique items: within each
“timbre × key × RR” group, all 3 velocity files are identical. All 252 duplicate
groups are duplicates across velocity zones; there are no exact duplicates
across keys, RR, or timbres. The project therefore accurately records
**1 genuinely recorded velocity, 3 upstream mapping zones, 2 RR variants, and
2 timbre groups**. Retaining all three mapping zones preserves upstream routing
compatibility and does not present them as three recorded dynamic layers.

`rootNote` in the upstream `clavichord.dspreset` is labeled 40–102, but the
recorded fundamentals correspond globally to MIDI 28–90 (E1–F#6), one octave
lower. The project preserves the original recording pitch, maps the formal key
range to 28–90, and does not raise all WAV files by 12 semitones.
`SIMPK调音表.json` records only per-path fine tuning relative to the actual
root; the original WAV bytes remain unchanged.

Frozen digests for the archive, preset, license, and all 756 paths, together
with the PCM uniqueness evidence above, are in
[`SIMPK来源证据.json`](SIMPK来源证据.json) and
[`样本质量核验.json`](样本质量核验.json). Digests of installed generated
artifacts are in [`资源核验.json`](资源核验.json). The installer uses frozen
digests to reject incomplete or substituted downloads.

## Comparison candidate: Edinburgh DataShare 1740s unfretted clavichord

- Persistent DOI: <https://doi.org/10.7488/ds/8090>
- License: CC BY 4.0
- Original archive SHA-256: `5018294c5f308a6c7693f695d834fc5d3a9ceda31a29b6ad7c25361a3e153548`
- Coverage: C2–D6, A4=415 Hz; four groups comprising original/denoised and without/with vibrato

This is a real historical instrument with a suitable license, but the publisher
explicitly records mains hum, missing notes, poor recording quality, and
denoising. It also has fewer valid root samples than keys and needs 3–4
semitones of stretching. After local extraction, path, and representative
tuning checks, it remains a research comparison rather than the current
runtime source.

## Replaced former entry

The previous version used VCSL's `TX81Z - Clavisynth.sfz`. Its CC0 provenance
and mapping are valid, but it is a Yamaha TX81Z FM synthesized timbre rather
than a clavichord recording, so it no longer serves as this instrument's
high-fidelity entry.
