# Banjo (itsclipping Ganjo six-string guitar-banjo)

This directory retains the project's “banjo” entry, but its formal source has
been replaced with itsclipping Ganjo: an SX-brand six-string guitar-banjo. It
reuses `tianlai/dedicated_sfz.py` as its rendering engine and has no silent
fallback to a general-purpose SoundFont.

## Mapping and articulation

- Default articulation: `normal`
- Mapping: `ganjo.sfz`
- Range: D2-C5 (MIDI 38-72)
- The frozen upstream mapping contains 58 regions and 58 independent samples. After the project mapping layer precisely excludes 3 outlier G♯4 alternates, 55 regions and 55 samples remain active, with 39 regions participating in round robin.
- Upstream has no genuine velocity layers; input velocity is shaped only by continuous gain in the renderer.
- Listening review found MIDI 47 (named `B3.wav` upstream) about 3.5 dB quieter than adjacent notes. `sample_gain_db_overrides` now adds `+3.2 dB` to this sample. This is a loudness correction: it neither replaces the sample nor changes its pitch, and it does not modify the pinned upstream resource tree.
- Listening review also found that the six MIDI 56 alternates (named G♯4 upstream) divide into two clearly different plucking textures: old RR1, RR5, and RR6 are brighter with conspicuous attacks, while old RR2, RR3, and RR4 are more consistent. `sample_region_exclusions` now excludes the first group and renumbers the retained three consecutively as a three-way round robin. The original CC0 WAV and SFZ files remain unchanged.

## Tuning

Per-sample pitch calibration is enabled at runtime
(`apply_pitch_calibration=true`). The calibration report was regenerated by
measuring all current 58 root samples: the median measured deviation from the
upstream mapping is +44.782 cents, and the maximum absolute deviation is
58.715 cents. Rendering corrects each sample by its own measured value; see
[`音准校准.json`](音准校准.json).

## Quality status

The single-instrument quality status is `formal`; ensemble use, real
repertoire, and automatic orchestration remain `untested`. Listening reports
for the former FlameStudios five-string source are not evidence for this
source, which must be rendered and reviewed anew.

## Known limitations

A six-string guitar-banjo is not a “higher-detail version” of a traditional
five-string banjo. It brings guitar-style six-string construction and playing
logic to a banjo resonator, without the traditional banjo's short fifth string
or its characteristic roll technique. This entry can therefore provide a
credible six-string guitar-banjo timbre, but it cannot stand in for a complete
traditional five-string banjo performance model.
