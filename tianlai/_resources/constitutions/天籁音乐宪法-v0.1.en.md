# Tianlai Music Constitution v0.1

> **Document status**: A non-normative creative reference retained by the project author and organized with AI assistance. It is not a legal instrument or technical contract, an automated quality gate, or a universal law of aesthetics; see the
> [directory README](README.en.md) for its scope of use and evidentiary boundaries. Adoption is entirely at the creator’s discretion; noncompliance does not trigger project penalties, license invalidation, or restrictions on engine functionality.

> **Nature**: A layered code of judgment for human creators, composition Agents, and music rendering systems.
> **Scale**: 9 vertical levels, 50 divisions of the code, and 538 single-sentence clauses.
> **Purpose**: Not to prescribe fixed answers for a work, but to protect identity, causality, emotion, style, and audible results, while making exceptions explainable, verifiable, and inheritable.

## 0. Instructions for Use

1. **Not all clauses are active at once.** Each work first establishes a C2 Work-Specific Charter, then activates the relevant C3 jurisdictions; normally only 20–60 clauses require focused execution.
2. **Vertical levels govern.** Higher law determines “why and who takes priority”; lower law determines “how it is realized.”
3. **Horizontal jurisdictions may overlap.** A work may simultaneously be governed by multiple bodies of special law concerning melody, groove, harmony, timbre, process, narrative, culture, and more.
4. **Conflicts are not resolved by averaging.** Protect technical usability and the work’s promises first, then rule according to the work’s sovereignty and the applicable jurisdictions.
5. **An exception is not a loophole.** Any violation must register its purpose, scope, the higher value protected, the cost paid, the method of recovery, and listening-test evidence.
6. **The ear has final authority.** Metrics provide evidence; they do not overturn stable, reproducible auditory judgment.

### 0.1 Vertical Levels

|Level|Name|Jurisdiction|
|-|-|-|
|C0|Meta-Constitution|Defines goals, methods of interpretation, conflict order, exceptions, and amendment; governs all levels.|
|C1|Universal Constitution|Defines the principles of perception, attention, memory, time, emotion, embodiment, culture, and performance that remain protected across styles.|
|C2|Work-Specific Charter|Turns abstract principles into the sovereignty, promises, prohibited zones, trajectories, scarce resources, and final-review metrics of one work.|
|C3|Special Jurisdictions|Handles differences through overlapping formulas of sovereignty, emotion, and style; it is not a mutually exclusive genre tree.|
|C4|Structural Law|Governs temporal levels, sectional functions, repetition and transformation, transitions, tension, climax, and ending.|
|C5|Grammatical Law|Governs motives, melody, rhythm, harmony, counterpoint, lyrics, and silence.|
|C6|Orchestration and Performance Law|Governs roles, timbre, register, instrumental nature, playability, expression, and space.|
|C7|Production and Release Law|Governs spectrum, masking, dynamics, transients, mixing, mastering, release, and technical quality control.|
|C8|Review and Evolution Law|Governs evaluation, listening tests, diagnosis, precedent, versions, amendment, and Agent execution.|

### 0.2 Types of Legal Effect

|Level|Nature of the Clauses|Effect|
|-|-|-|
|C0|Meta-Constitution|Highest constraint; it may be departed from temporarily only through an explicitly registered, proportionate constitutional exception.|
|C1|Universal Constitution|Default cross-style constraint; lower law may only elaborate it and may not silently conflict with it.|
|C2|Mandatory Work-Specific Declaration|Must be completed for every work; the completed declaration becomes a direct source of law for that work.|
|C3|Activated Special Law|Takes effect when activated by C2; multiple jurisdictions may coexist and intersect.|
|C4–C6|Presumptively Applicable Implementing Law|Acts as a strong default; it may be overridden with reasons by a more specific C2/C3 provision.|
|C7|Technical and Release Implementing Law|Technical safety is comparatively strict; aesthetic production remains governed by C2/C3.|
|C8|Procedural Law|Defines procedures for review, listening tests, precedent, amendment, and Agent execution.|

### 0.3 Order of Effect and Conflict

**C0 > C1 > C2 > C3 > C4 > C5 > C6 > C7 > the technical provisions of C8.**

When clauses conflict, protect the following in order:

**Technical usability → work-specific promises → primary sovereignty → perceptibility → long-range structure → stylistic credibility → local beauty.**

For conflicts at the same level, give priority to: **the more specific law, the newer and more thoroughly validated precedent, and the lower-cost, reversible solution.**

### 0.4 Creative Execution Chain

```text
Natural-language intent
  ↓
C2 Work-Specific Charter: sovereignty / emotion vector / motion vector / style formula / prohibited zones
  ↓
C3 activated special jurisdictions: melody / groove / harmony / timbre / process / narrative / culture…
  ↓
C4 structure: temporal levels / sectional functions / tension and climax
  ↓
C5 grammar: motive / melody / rhythm / harmony / counterpoint / silence
  ↓
C6 orchestration and performance → C7 production and release
  ↓
C8 multi-version review / listening tests / precedent / revision
```

### 0.5 Registration of a Lawful Exception

```yaml
exception:
  violated_clause: clause number
  purpose: musical purpose the exception is intended to achieve
  scope: start and end times and affected parts
  higher_value: higher-order value being protected
  cost: cost to clarity/style/structure/technology, etc.
  recovery: how the work acknowledges or retracts this transgression
  evidence: A/B, repeat listening, audience, or performer evidence
  reusable: whether it may become precedent
```

## 2. Complete Provisions

### C0 Meta-Constitution: Supreme Principles of Musical Governance

* **C0.01｜Experiential objective**: A work should form a sustainable overall experience; it need not aim to make every second maximally comfortable.
* **C0.02｜Vector evaluation**: “Sounding good” must be decomposed into dimensions such as audibility, learnability, credibility, emotional force, repeat-listening value, and contextual fit, rather than compressed into one score.
* **C0.03｜Auditory final review**: Theory, metrics, and models provide evidence only; listening impressions that recur under controlled conditions have final authority.
* **C0.04｜Context first**: Every judgment must state its audience, situation, medium, cultural background, and monitoring conditions.
* **C0.05｜Declaration of sovereignty**: Every work must designate its primary sovereignty: melody, groove, harmony, counterpoint, timbre, process, narrative, text, or interaction.
* **C0.06｜Order of power**: Primary sovereignty may govern secondary sovereignties; a secondary sovereignty must not unintentionally seize core attention.
* **C0.07｜Promises first**: A work’s high-level promises outweigh local pleasantness, virtuosity, stylistic purity, and attractive parameter values.
* **C0.08｜Temporal causality**: A major change must be prepared, remembered, or leave consequences afterward.
* **C0.09｜Continuity of identity**: Traceable identity should remain after change; a complete rupture must be declared as an event in the work.
* **C0.10｜Finite resources**: Attention, memory, spectrum, dynamics, register, and novelty are all finite budgets.
* **C0.11｜Many causes, one effect**: No single parameter may monopolize the explanation of emotion, style, or good sound.
* **C0.12｜Style as probability**: Style is a distribution of features and a network of relationships, not a fixed checklist.
* **C0.13｜Cultural relativity**: Apart from physiological boundaries of hearing, no culture-specific grammar may be passed off as universal truth.
* **C0.14｜Overlapping jurisdictions**: One work may simultaneously be governed by multiple stylistic, emotional, media, and cultural jurisdictions.
* **C0.15｜Inheritance from higher law**: Lower-level rules inherit higher-level rules by default; they may elaborate them but may not silently conflict with them.
* **C0.16｜Registration of exceptions**: A violation must state its purpose, scope, cost, method of recovery, and expected auditory benefit.
* **C0.17｜Principle of proportionality**: A local violation may be permitted if it protects a higher value; the extent of the transgression must be proportionate to the meaning gained.
* **C0.18｜Lawful roughness**: Noise, distortion, dissonance, mechanicality, and emptiness are all lawful when they perform a defined function.
* **C0.19｜Distinguishing defects**: Technical defects such as clipping, masking, and uncontrolled resonance must not masquerade as aesthetics unless deliberately designed and stably reproducible.
* **C0.20｜Separation of generation and review**: The generator proposes possibilities; the reviewer identifies sacrifices; they must not share one conservative objective.
* **C0.21｜Preserving multiple solutions**: The system should retain multiple Pareto candidates rather than prematurely average them into one safe answer.
* **C0.22｜Anti-metric gaming**: Any metric optimized to the point of harming the listening experience forfeits its authority.
* **C0.23｜Accumulation of precedent**: Effective decisions should record context, conflict, choice, result, and scope of applicability.
* **C0.24｜Cautious amendment**: The Constitution is amended only on repeated evidence across works and jurisdictions; success in a single work enters precedent first.
* **C0.25｜Protection of the unknown**: The system must preserve exploratory space beyond the rules and must not treat historical averages as the boundaries of creation.
* **C0.26｜Order in conflict**: In a conflict, protect in order: technical usability → the work’s promises → primary sovereignty → perceptibility → long-range structure → stylistic credibility → local beauty.

### C1.1 Universal Constitution: Perceptibility

* **C1.1.01｜Discernible protagonist**: Primary information must be consistently identifiable on target devices at normal volume.
* **C1.1.02｜Duty to yield**: A non-protagonist must not unintentionally mask the protagonist at the same moment, register, frequency band, and envelope.
* **C1.1.03｜Loudness neutrality**: Versions must be loudness-matched before comparison; louder must not automatically be judged better.
* **C1.1.04｜Spectrum with cause**: Brightness, darkness, thickness, and thinness must serve role and emotion rather than an abstract ideal of “flatness.”
* **C1.1.05｜Proportionate roughness**: Roughness and harmonicity are interpreted according to style, register, timbre, and context; there is no absolute threshold of pleasantness.
* **C1.1.06｜Readable attack**: Attack, sustain, and decay must support beat, articulation, and instrumental identity.
* **C1.1.07｜Low-frequency allocation**: Sub-bass, kick, bass, and low parts must have clearly divided authority and avoid jointly consuming all headroom.
* **C1.1.08｜Readable dynamics**: Key layers must remain discernible after dynamic changes; compression must not flatten syntax.
* **C1.1.09｜Perceptible boundaries**: Important phrases, sections, and state transitions must have enough cues to be heard.
* **C1.1.10｜Lawful emptiness**: Silence, negative space, and sparseness may carry structure and must not be filled without purpose.
* **C1.1.11｜Translation across playback systems**: Core identity must survive on headphones, small speakers, mono, or the target playback system.
* **C1.1.12｜Auditory safety**: Sustained harshness, excessive true peaks, and dangerous sound pressure must not substitute for intensity.

### C1.2 Universal Constitution: Attention and Hierarchy

* **C1.2.01｜Clear focus**: In principle, each moment has only one primary focus of attention; multiple foci must be intentional polyphony.
* **C1.2.02｜Three-plane hierarchy**: The foreground carries primary information, the middle ground carries relationships, and the background carries environment and continuity.
* **C1.2.03｜Limit simultaneous novelty**: A new melody, timbre, rhythm, and harmony should not all appear for the first time together.
* **C1.2.04｜Accountability for density**: Before adding a part, state the new information, emotion, or function it contributes; if there is no answer, delete it.
* **C1.2.05｜Accompaniment keeps its office**: Accompaniment should support, counterpose, or obstruct the protagonist rather than merely persist.
* **C1.2.06｜Entrances and exits have cause**: A part’s entrance or exit must change attention, energy, meaning, or space.
* **C1.2.07｜Scarcity marks importance**: The highest pitch, greatest loudness, brightest timbre, and densest texture should gain meaning through scarcity.
* **C1.2.08｜Stagger complexity**: When one dimension is complex, at least one adjacent dimension should provide a stable anchor.
* **C1.2.09｜Contrast needs a baseline**: A deviation cannot be expected to become contrast automatically unless a norm has first been established.
* **C1.2.10｜Coordination across scales**: Local focus must serve the phrase, the phrase must serve the section, and the section must serve the whole work.

### C1.3 Universal Constitution: Identity, Memory, and Expectation

* **C1.3.01｜Core of recognition**: A work must have at least one traceable identity core: contour, rhythm, interval, timbre, harmony, or process.
* **C1.3.02｜Declaration of invariants**: Every transformation must retain at least one invariant so the listener can recognize kinship.
* **C1.3.03｜Establish before transforming**: Important material should in principle appear first in readable form before being fragmented, masked, or reinterpreted.
* **C1.3.04｜License to repeat**: Repetition establishes a model; variation proves that repetition remains worthwhile.
* **C1.3.05｜Exact repetition**: Exact repetition may establish identity at first; later repetitions should choose fidelity or variation according to purpose.
* **C1.3.06｜Recurrence across intervals**: Key material must return across time so memory participates in structure instead of relying only on adjacent repetition.
* **C1.3.07｜Changed return**: A return must not merely copy the past; it must change at least role, context, consequence, or mode of hearing.
* **C1.3.08｜Few landmarks**: Distinctive registers, chords, timbres, and pauses should be used sparingly and bound to key nodes.
* **C1.3.09｜A model can be built**: Listeners must receive enough regularity to form predictions; sustained lack of rules constitutes only noise.
* **C1.3.10｜Contextual surprise**: The magnitude of surprise must match the uncertainty at that moment; high uncertainty calls for anchors, while low uncertainty permits abrupt change.
* **C1.3.11｜Localized complexity**: High information density should be concentrated in bridges, fills, climaxes, or specific jurisdictions rather than loaded throughout.
* **C1.3.12｜Audience calibration**: The complexity budget must be adjusted for the target audience’s familiarity with the style and the repeat-listening context.
* **C1.3.13｜Reward after learning**: After identity has been learned, a work should continue to offer relationships, performance, timbre, or long-range correspondences.
* **C1.3.14｜Using forgetting**: Moderate delay, masking, and incomplete recollection can create the pleasure of recognition.

### C1.4 Universal Constitution: Time, Form, and Causality

* **C1.4.01｜Multiscale organization**: Events, figures, motives, phrases, periods, sections, and the entire work may be nested layer by layer.
* **C1.4.02｜Function at every level**: Every level that exists must have a boundary, identity, or function and must not be invented merely for neatness.
* **C1.4.03｜Sections have functions**: A section must perform one of establishment, advance, deviation, rupture, return, climax, or aftermath.
* **C1.4.04｜Phrases breathe**: A phrase must have an initiation, continuation, arrival, or intentional refusal to arrive.
* **C1.4.05｜Lawful transitions**: A transition must either be gradually prepared or explicitly act as a rupture; an ambiguous mishap is weakest.
* **C1.4.06｜Rate of information**: Perceived speed is determined jointly by event rate, harmonic rhythm, envelope, and motion; it is not equivalent to BPM.
* **C1.4.07｜Separate energy**: Energy, tension, loudness, speed, and density must be modeled separately.
* **C1.4.08｜Earned climax**: A climax must be earned through scarcity, accumulation, recovery, or conflict resolution, not only through added layers.
* **C1.4.09｜Many forms of climax**: A climax may consist of silence, harmonic arrival, exposed timbre, rhythmic pause, or revelation of meaning.
* **C1.4.10｜Equivalent release**: The intensity of release should answer the preceding accumulation; premature, excessive, or consequence-free release loses value.
* **C1.4.11｜Duty of aftermath**: After a major event, leave an aftermath that acknowledges it occurred, unless abrupt termination is itself the conclusion.
* **C1.4.12｜The ending answers**: An ending must fulfill, rewrite, suspend, or reject the opening promise; it cannot merely stop.
* **C1.4.13｜Even stasis moves**: Static, ambient, and impersonal music must still undergo microchange in spectrum, space, texture, or attention.
* **C1.4.14｜Sustaining loops**: Loop-based music must make seams natural and preserve within the loop a sustainable arc rather than one exhausted after a single pass.

### C1.5 Universal Constitution: Emotion and Narrative

* **C1.5.01｜Vector description**: Emotion must record at least valence, arousal, motion, tension, agency, intimacy, scale, temporal orientation, and sense of closure.
* **C1.5.02｜Three-way distinction**: Distinguish what the music expresses, what the listener feels, and what the passage does in the narrative.
* **C1.5.03｜Tempo is not a verdict**: Tempo chiefly affects motion and arousal; it does not determine happiness or sadness by itself.
* **C1.5.04｜Mode is not a verdict**: Major, minor, pentatonic, modal, and atonal materials provide cues only; they do not determine emotion by themselves.
* **C1.5.05｜Agency enters the law**: Even within sadness, struggle, flight, mourning, numbness, and acceptance require different logics of motion and closure.
* **C1.5.06｜Closure enters the law**: The same material may change its narrative conclusion through resolution, partial resolution, deceptive resolution, or refusal to resolve.
* **C1.5.07｜Temporal orientation**: Memory, the present, and premonition must be distinguished through timbral distance, modes of repetition, and syntactic direction.
* **C1.5.08｜Intimacy and scale**: Close-miked whispering and grand sublimity must not be distinguished by volume alone; space, register, and group size must also change.
* **C1.5.09｜Lawful mixtures**: Bittersweetness, tragic heroism, uncanny beauty, and unease within warmth should arise from conflicting dimensions.
* **C1.5.10｜The curve takes priority**: Emotion should move through time; a fixed label for the whole work can only be a substrate.
* **C1.5.11｜Causal narrative**: Emotional change must be triggered, accumulated, or revealed by musical events rather than switch presets without cause.
* **C1.5.12｜Negative emotions have vitality**: Sad does not mean slow, dark, and low; positive does not mean fast, bright, and large.
* **C1.5.13｜Boundary of tragic heroism**: Tragic heroism retains cost and unfinished business; triumph gives stable belonging and greater closure.
* **C1.5.14｜Duty of evidence**: Every emotion word must correspond to at least two independent kinds of musical evidence.

### C1.6 Universal Constitution: Embodiment, Groove, and Motion

* **C1.6.01｜Pulse anchoring**: A groove jurisdiction must give the body a predictable temporal anchor.
* **C1.6.02｜Relative deviation**: Syncopation exists only against stable beats; unanchored deviation does not constitute groove.
* **C1.6.03｜Three-layer division of labor**: Rhythmic layers should be divided into steady beat, propulsion, and provocation, avoiding virtuosity in every layer at once.
* **C1.6.04｜Moderation first**: For broadly embodied drive, try moderate syncopation first, then adjust for style and audience.
* **C1.6.05｜Directed microtiming**: Humanization must shift according to beat position, syntax, and style, not use independent random jitter.
* **C1.6.06｜Low-frequency compact**: Kick and bass must clearly relate by coincidence, displacement, call and response, or avoidance.
* **C1.6.07｜Rests exert force**: Groove is shaped jointly by sound and gaps; a rest can propel more strongly than an added note.
* **C1.6.08｜Quantization has style**: Absolute alignment, laid-back placement, pushing ahead, and swing are all lawful, but must be globally coherent.
* **C1.6.09｜Motion vector**: Motion must jointly record the main beat, subdivision, attack rate, harmonic rhythm, envelope, and textural flow rate.
* **C1.6.10｜Fit to use**: Dancing, focused listening, meditation, and narrative scoring use different budgets of embodied complexity.

### C1.7 Universal Constitution: Style, Culture, and Authenticity

* **C1.7.01｜References as sources of law**: Stylistic judgments should derive from multiple works, authors, and periods, not be represented by one work.
* **C1.7.02｜Selective core**: Distinguish core, common, and ornamental markers of a style; avoid mechanically selecting all of them.
* **C1.7.03｜Mixing by dimension**: Fusion music should state which jurisdiction governs melody, rhythm, harmony, timbre, and form respectively.
* **C1.7.04｜Period enters the law**: The production, performance, and dynamic aesthetics of one genre in different periods must not be conflated.
* **C1.7.05｜Against tokenism**: Cultural identity cannot rest only on one instrument, one scale system, or surface timbral markers.
* **C1.7.06｜Grammar as a set**: When citing a cultural vocabulary, consider syntax, ornament, breathing, timbre, time, and social function together.
* **C1.7.07｜A bridge to the unfamiliar**: For an unfamiliar audience, retain at least one accessible anchor of beat, contour, emotion, or structure.
* **C1.7.08｜Knowable deviation**: When crossing a stylistic boundary, make the deviation part of the work’s identity rather than a sign of inadequate training.
* **C1.7.09｜Regional specificity**: Broad categories such as “Chinese style,” “classical,” and “electronic” may be entry points only; they cannot replace a specific jurisdiction.
* **C1.7.10｜Culture can revise**: Audience and cultural feedback may adjust stylistic law, but must not retroactively rewrite universal auditory defects.

### C1.8 Universal Constitution: Performance and Vitality

* **C1.8.01｜Performance is not decoration**: Timing, dynamics, articulation, connection and separation, and timbral change belong to the work’s grammar.
* **C1.8.02｜Syntactic shaping**: Expressive change must form direction across notes rather than randomize each note.
* **C1.8.03｜Bodily feasibility**: Breathing, bowing, fingering, embouchure, striking, and resonance constraints must enter the composition.
* **C1.8.04｜Instrumental dignity**: An instrument should have a role suited to its sound production, register, and inertia; sound sources are not interchangeable textures.
* **C1.8.05｜Ornament has grammar**: Trills, vibrato, glissandi, grace notes, and accents must obey style and syntax.
* **C1.8.06｜Lawful mechanics**: Mechanical precision may serve the aesthetics of minimalism, electronic music, coldness, or superhuman scale.
* **C1.8.07｜Restrained microdifference**: Perceptible microdifference is preferable to meaningless jitter; change should concentrate at key positions.
* **C1.8.08｜Reserve performer authority**: When intended for human performance, reasonable interpretive space should remain rather than fixing every expression.
* **C1.8.09｜Audible structure**: Performance should help listeners hear hierarchy, boundaries, accents, and the long line, not merely add emotion.
* **C1.8.10｜Faithful rendering**: High-quality rendering exists to realize the work’s judgments, not to conceal hollow writing with realism.

### C2 Work-Specific Charter: Questions Every Work Must Answer First

* **C2.01｜One-sentence promise**: State in one sentence what the work will cause to change, and when.
* **C2.02｜Target audience**: Specify general listeners, trained listeners, a particular community, or personal use.
* **C2.03｜Use context**: Specify standalone listening, short video, stage, film and television, game, meditation, or another medium.
* **C2.04｜Duration scale**: Specify the target duration and principal structural levels.
* **C2.05｜Primary sovereignty**: Choose only one primary sovereignty and state what it may not sacrifice.
* **C2.06｜Secondary sovereignties**: List at most three secondary sovereignties and their relations of subordination.
* **C2.07｜Jurisdiction formula**: List the activated stylistic, emotional, cultural, and media jurisdictions and their weights.
* **C2.08｜Emotional trajectory**: Define emotional vectors separately for the opening, turn, climax, and ending.
* **C2.09｜Motion trajectory**: Define perceived speed, attack rate, harmonic rhythm, and textural flow rate separately.
* **C2.10｜Identity core**: Designate one to three memorable motives, timbres, harmonies, or processes.
* **C2.11｜Invariants**: Declare the contour, rhythm, intervals, timbre, or function that must remain when the identity core is transformed.
* **C2.12｜Right of transformation**: Declare the permitted transposition, inversion, augmentation and diminution, slicing, reorchestration, reharmonization, and role exchange.
* **C2.13｜Prohibited shortcuts**: List cheap devices on which the work may not rely, such as unmotivated layering, constant high loudness, or a stock ending.
* **C2.14｜Formal map**: State the sectional order, hierarchical boundaries, and function of each section.
* **C2.15｜Energy map**: Plot energy, tension, density, loudness, and register as independent curves.
* **C2.16｜Landmark list**: Limit scarce events such as the highest pitch, greatest density, unique chord, and longest silence.
* **C2.17｜Climactic authority**: Define which rules the climax may violate, how many times it may occur, and what consequences follow.
* **C2.18｜Type of ending**: Choose fulfillment, rewriting, openness, decay, loop, rupture, or resonance.
* **C2.19｜Novelty budget**: Define how much new material each section may introduce simultaneously.
* **C2.20｜Density budget**: Define how many active parts, occupied frequency bands, and rhythmic layers each section permits.
* **C2.21｜Role table**: Assign each part a function of principal statement, support, counterpose, resistance, echo, environment, or connection.
* **C2.22｜Spatial world**: Define front and back, near and far, width, dryness and wetness, and how space changes with the narrative.
* **C2.23｜Production aesthetic**: Define targets such as clean, warm, rough, transparent, oppressive, retro, or surreal.
* **C2.24｜List of exceptions**: Register acceptable violations and unacceptable failures in advance.
* **C2.25｜Final-review metrics**: Select only the five to eight evaluation dimensions most important to this work; averaging them all is prohibited.

### C3.1A Melody-Sovereignty Jurisdiction M｜Pop Ballads, Folk, Neoclassical, Theme Music

* **C3.M.01｜Works when sung bare**: The principal melody should retain its identity of contour, rhythm, and arrival without arrangement.
* **C3.M.02｜Contour first**: Design overall direction and phrase peaks before filling in local pitches.
* **C3.M.03｜Stepwise motion as foundation**: A memorable melody defaults to neighboring motion as its skeleton; leaps serve signatures, turns, or keywords.
* **C3.M.04｜Recovering a leap**: A prominent leap should be explained by contrary motion, repetition, harmonic support, or rhythmic emphasis.
* **C3.M.05｜Accompaniment yields authority**: Harmony, rhythm, and timbre must not blur the accents and breathing of the principal melody.
* **C3.M.06｜Scarce phrase peaks**: The highest note, longest note, and greatest leap should not proliferate indiscriminately.
* **C3.M.07｜Recurrence in memory**: The principal hook must return before the listener forgets it and undergo a small change after it has been learned.

### C3.1B Groove-Sovereignty Jurisdiction G｜Funk, Hip-hop, House, Disco, Dance Music

* **C3.G.01｜The body understands first**: The body must grasp the main pulse and low-frequency relationship before ornament.
* **C3.G.02｜Credible loop**: After many repetitions, a core loop should remain alive through microtiming, timbre, or changes in layering.
* **C3.G.03｜Anchored syncopation**: Offbeat placement must create completable tension against stable beat positions.
* **C3.G.04｜Low-frequency sovereignty**: Timing relationships among kick, bass, and principal riff form the core of the jurisdiction.
* **C3.G.05｜Harmony may be static**: Simple or static harmony is lawful when relationships inside the groove continue to develop.
* **C3.G.06｜Clear transients**: Attacks, rests, and dynamic yielding matter more than endless layering.
* **C3.G.07｜Breaks must pay off**: A break, drop, or pause must strengthen the return rather than merely remove parts.

### C3.1C Harmony-Sovereignty Jurisdiction H｜Jazz, Gospel, Neo-soul, Romanticism, Impressionism

* **C3.H.01｜Trajectory over chord**: Identity comes from how harmony departs, suspends, and returns, not from one complex chord.
* **C3.H.02｜Voice leading**: Quality of connection takes priority over chord labels; common tones, chromatic lines, and bass direction must be audible.
* **C3.H.03｜Voicing enters the law**: Voicing, register, density, and timbre jointly determine harmonic color.
* **C3.H.04｜Bass gives direction**: Roots, inversions, pedals, and bass melodies must carry structural direction.
* **C3.H.05｜Extensions have functions**: Extensions, substitute chords, and borrowings must carry color, tension, or meaning.
* **C3.H.06｜Melody may be simple**: Melody may leave space or repeat so harmonic change becomes the principal event.
* **C3.H.07｜Resolution by degree**: Full, partial, deceptive, and delayed resolutions should be distributed according to narrative need.

### C3.1D Counterpoint-Sovereignty Jurisdiction P｜Baroque, Fugue, Choral, Polyphonic Chamber Music

* **C3.P.01｜Voices have intention**: Each principal voice must have an independently traceable contour, rhythm, or task.
* **C3.P.02｜Independence is not equal authority**: Voices may be independent, but focus must shift over time instead of being permanently contested.
* **C3.P.03｜Discernible imitation**: Subject, answer, inversion, augmentation and diminution, and canonic relations must be audible.
* **C3.P.04｜Collisions have resolution**: Dissonance should arise from voice movement and acquire a stylistically internal destination.
* **C3.P.05｜Phased density**: A new voice must change the hierarchy, not merely make the same information thicker.
* **C3.P.06｜Division of register**: Voice crossing and overlap require expressive reasons; discernibility is the default.
* **C3.P.07｜Consensus at cadence**: At important cadences, multiple voices must form a shared structural judgment.

### C3.1E Timbre–Space-Sovereignty Jurisdiction T｜Ambient, Drone, Spectral, Shoegaze, Industrial, Soundscape

* **C3.T.01｜Timbre is syntax**: Changes in spectrum, envelope, noise, distortion, and space may carry theme and development.
* **C3.T.02｜Lawful pitch stasis**: Pitch and harmony may remain static for long periods when textural relationships continue to change.
* **C3.T.03｜Color changes have a course**: Timbral change must have direction, thresholds, and memory, not disorderly preset rotation.
* **C3.T.04｜Space has roles**: Distance, width, reflections, and reverb tails should alter narrative position.
* **C3.T.05｜Roughness can be beautiful**: Distortion and noise must have identity, layers, and controllable boundaries.
* **C3.T.06｜Spectral windows**: Even dense textures must leave transient, frequency-band, or spatial windows for key events.
* **C3.T.07｜Perceptible slow change**: The speed of a gradient must let the target audience perceive change without losing immersion.

### C3.1F Process-Sovereignty Jurisdiction O｜Minimalism, Phase Music, Generative Music, Algorithmic Music

* **C3.O.01｜Audible process**: Listeners should gradually perceive how the rules operate unless the hidden process is itself the theme.
* **C3.O.02｜Restrained materials**: Generate rich time from a few cells, parameters, or probabilities instead of constantly replacing material.
* **C3.O.03｜Continuous change**: Microvariation must accumulate into macroscopic difference and leave a traceable path.
* **C3.O.04｜Few exceptions**: The fewer abrupt changes there are, the greater their structural power.
* **C3.O.05｜Local autonomy**: Processes may run at different speeds in different parts, but perceptible relationships must exist.
* **C3.O.06｜Reasoned termination**: An ending should arise from exhaustion, alignment, a threshold, freezing, or intentional interruption of the process.
* **C3.O.07｜Against mechanical monotony**: Mechanical accuracy does not mean absence of hierarchy; register, density, phase, and timbre must still be organized.

### C3.1G Narrative–Energy-Sovereignty Jurisdiction N｜Film, Games, Post-rock, Symphonic Poems, Epic Scores

* **C3.N.01｜Mapping to state**: Each section must correspond to a state of character, world, conflict, action, or viewpoint.
* **C3.N.02｜Themes have roles**: A motive may represent not only melody but also a character, place, relationship, or fate.
* **C3.N.03｜Narrative transformation**: Changes in a theme’s orchestration, harmony, speed, and completeness should reflect changes in character.
* **C3.N.04｜The long line first**: A local climax must serve the larger arc of the work or scene.
* **C3.N.05｜Climax has a price**: Maximum energy must fulfill prior causality and leave loss, resolution, or a new situation.
* **C3.N.06｜Quiet sections do something**: A low-energy section should carry foreshadowing, aftermath, a change of viewpoint, or internal change.
* **C3.N.07｜Restrained synchronization**: Synchronization points serve the narrative; do not mechanically score every action.

### C3.1H Text–Persona-Sovereignty Jurisdiction L｜Pop Songs, Rap, Musical Theatre, Singer-Songwriter Music

* **C3.L.01｜Meaning first**: Melody, rhythm, and harmony must protect keywords, tone, and narrative viewpoint.
* **C3.L.02｜Isomorphic prosody**: Linguistic stress and syllable length should in principle align with melodic stress; displacement requires an expressive purpose.
* **C3.L.03｜Singable boundaries**: Range, breathing, diction, and vowel placement must suit the target voice.
* **C3.L.04｜Stable persona**: Singing stance, wording, timbre, and arrangement must belong to the same narrator.
* **C3.L.05｜Leave space for words**: Competing parts should be reduced before and after important lines so meaning can land.
* **C3.L.06｜Double hook**: A chorus hook must have both a verbal and a musical point of memory.
* **C3.L.07｜Responsive accompaniment**: An arrangement may comment on, ironize, or support the text; it should not be merely an emotional backdrop.

### C3.1I Ritual–Group-Sovereignty Jurisdiction R｜Hymns, Marches, Folk Dance, Festivals, Collective Electronic Music

* **C3.R.01｜Entering together**: Beat, slogan, melody, or movement must facilitate group synchronization.
* **C3.R.02｜Justified repetition**: High repetition may be exchanged for participation, memory, and a sense of community.
* **C3.R.03｜Response structure**: Lead–response, call–tutti, or individual–group relations should be clear.
* **C3.R.04｜Accessible range**: For singing or participation, core material must lie within a range accessible to most people.
* **C3.R.05｜Shared climax**: The climax should enlarge shared movement, shared parts, or shared closure.
* **C3.R.06｜Credible identity**: Ritual markers must match the actual culture, situation, and group function.

### C3.1J Cultural-Vocabulary-Sovereignty Jurisdiction C｜Regional Traditions, Pentatonic Vocabulary, Folk Instruments, Cross-Cultural Fusion

* **C3.C.01｜Specific naming**: Identify the particular tradition, region, period, and use before invoking a broad cultural label.
* **C3.C.02｜Systemic citation**: Scale, mode, ornament, syntax, rhythm, timbre, and space should be understood as an integrated set.
* **C3.C.03｜Instrumental nature**: Slides, *yin-nao* ornamentation, phrasing breaths, free rhythm, resonance, and related features must begin from the instrument’s embodiment.
* **C3.C.04｜Translation across systems**: When combining with Western harmony or electronic production, state which system governs and which yields.
* **C3.C.05｜Avoid labeling**: A pentatonic scale plus one ethnic instrument does not automatically constitute a credible cultural style.
* **C3.C.06｜Negative space enters the law**: The emptiness, resonance, and flexible time of many traditions must not be flattened by the modern impulse to fill.
* **C3.C.07｜Cultural review**: Key stylistic judgments should be reviewed by listeners or performers familiar with the tradition.

### C3.1K Experimental–Anti-Grammar-Sovereignty Jurisdiction X｜Noise, Glitch, Avant-garde, Free Improvisation

* **C3.X.01｜The question comes first**: An experiment must pose an auditory question; being “different” cannot substitute for a work.
* **C3.X.02｜Local legislation**: Even when overturning conventions, establish a few local rules for listeners to trace.
* **C3.X.03｜Discernible destruction**: The norm being destroyed must first exist or be recognizable through cultural memory.
* **C3.X.04｜Accountability of materials**: Randomness, noise, microtones, and glitches must perform a structural or perceptual function.
* **C3.X.05｜Boundaries of lost control**: A system may simulate loss of control, but the creator must control entry, duration, and exit.
* **C3.X.06｜Aesthetics does not excuse**: The experimental jurisdiction remains subject to review for auditory safety, technical usability, and the work’s promises.

### C3.1L Interactive–Adaptive-Sovereignty Jurisdiction I｜Games, Installations, Variable Scores, Agent Music

* **C3.I.01｜State mapping**: Musical parameters must correspond to observable states rather than switch randomly.
* **C3.I.02｜Continuous transitions**: Horizontal resequencing, vertical layering, and transition passages must preserve tonal, metric, and timbral continuity.
* **C3.I.03｜Durable loops**: A loop unit must not repeat a one-time climax forever.
* **C3.I.04｜Tolerance of latency**: Transition design must tolerate trigger latency, player dwell time, and unpredictable duration.
* **C3.I.05｜Autonomous stems**: Each stem must work independently and, in combination, neither mask nor seize authority.
* **C3.I.06｜Memory across states**: Core identity should remain traceable across game states and orchestral levels.
* **C3.I.07｜Explainable agency**: For every change, an Agent must be able to state the triggering state, applicable rule, and sacrificed dimension.

### C3.2 Emotion Jurisdiction: Emotions with the Same Name Must Be Typed

* **C3.E.01｜Fast and sad / tragic advance**: Low valence, high arousal, high motion, medium-high agency, low closure; use a sustained engine and delayed resolution, and prohibit cost-free triumph.
* **C3.E.02｜Slow and sad / mourning**: Low valence, low arousal, low motion, low agency, high intimacy; use long tones, sparseness, and slow harmony, and prohibit hollow deceleration.
* **C3.E.03｜Tragic heroism**: Low valence, high arousal, large scale, high agency, partial closure; heroic expansion is allowed, but loss and lingering pain must remain.
* **C3.E.04｜Triumph**: High valence, high arousal, high agency, high closure; reinforce belonging, a shared beat, and definite arrival.
* **C3.E.05｜Nostalgia / bittersweetness**: Mixed valence, medium-low arousal, past orientation, medium closure; let familiar material return as incomplete, distant, or reharmonized.
* **C3.E.06｜Tenderness / intimacy**: Positive or bittersweet, low arousal, high intimacy, small scale; favor proximity, soft attacks, breathing, and restrained dynamics.
* **C3.E.07｜Serenity / transcendence**: Neutral to positive, low arousal, low agency, open closure, large space; maintain stable gravity and slow microvariation.
* **C3.E.08｜Suspense / unease**: Uncertain valence, medium-high tension, low stability, low closure; sustain the question through an ambiguous center, incomplete syntax, and irregular time.
* **C3.E.09｜Anger / aggression**: Low valence, high arousal, high agency, high roughness; use hard transients, repeated pressure, and controlled distortion while preserving rhythmic readability.
* **C3.E.10｜Sublimity / awe**: Mixed valence, medium-high arousal, immense scale, low personal agency; use wide register, vast space, slow changes of mass, and scarce extremes.
* **C3.E.11｜Uncanniness / alienation**: Unstable valence, medium arousal, low familiarity, high ambiguity; introduce impossible details into familiar structures rather than making everything random.
* **C3.E.12｜Cheerfulness / buoyancy**: High valence, high arousal, high agency, medium-high closure; use a clear pulse, upward impetus, and short feedback, avoiding reliance on brightness and loudness alone.
* **C3.E.13｜Dream / ethereality**: Mixed valence, low-medium arousal, low gravity, high openness; soften attacks, blur the center, and expand space while retaining landmarks for memory.
* **C3.E.14｜Resolve / farewell**: Low to mixed valence, medium-high arousal, high agency, high irreversibility; use a clear rhythm of action and an irretrievable termination, with no turning back at the end.

### C4.1 Structural Law: Temporal Levels

* **C4.1.01｜Sound event**: A single attack, sustain, noise, or silence is the smallest locatable event.
* **C4.1.02｜Gesture**: Several events form one rise, fall, impact, breath, or swing.
* **C4.1.03｜Cell**: The smallest repeatable relationship, formed by rhythm, interval, timbre, or harmony.
* **C4.1.04｜Motive**: A cell or combination of cells that can be recognized, transformed, and carry identity.
* **C4.1.05｜Phrase**: A short statement with direction, center of gravity, and boundary.
* **C4.1.06｜Period**: Several phrases forming a relatively complete unit around a local question.
* **C4.1.07｜Section**: A medium-timescale region with an independent function, energy, and orchestral state.
* **C4.1.08｜Movement / whole work**: The highest temporal unit integrating sections and carrying the overall promise and final recovery.
* **C4.1.09｜Omissible levels**: A short work may omit the period or movement, but not a perceptible relationship between local and whole.
* **C4.1.10｜Boundary strength**: The higher the level, the more dimensions usually need to cue its boundary together.

### C4.2 Structural Law: Sectional Functions

* **C4.2.01｜Introduction**: Establish the sonic world, gravity, pulse, or suspense without necessarily presenting the theme in full immediately.
* **C4.2.02｜Exposition**: Establish principal identity and basic rules in their most readable form.
* **C4.2.03｜Confirmation**: Prove through repetition or response that the material deserves to be remembered.
* **C4.2.04｜Advance**: Increase direction, potentially changing density, register, harmonic rhythm, or event rate.
* **C4.2.05｜Recasting**: Retain the identity core while changing role, orchestration, viewpoint, mode, or center of gravity.
* **C4.2.06｜Contrast**: Introduce a new state to illuminate old material rather than merely replacing one song with another.
* **C4.2.07｜Suspension**: Reduce external motion while retaining an unresolved question.
* **C4.2.08｜Rupture**: Deliberately sever rules, pulse, sound field, or completeness of identity.
* **C4.2.09｜Return**: Let old material reappear within a new history.
* **C4.2.10｜Concentration of energy**: Reduce possible directions and focus multiple tensions on one critical point.
* **C4.2.11｜Climax**: Fulfill, reveal, reverse, or reject the core promise.
* **C4.2.12｜Aftermath**: Let the meaning of the climax settle and reevaluate the remaining material.
* **C4.2.13｜Coda**: Decide how the world continues, disappears, loops, or closes.
* **C4.2.14｜Uniqueness of function**: Adjacent sections may share materials, but should not share exactly the same function for long.

### C4.3 Structural Law: Repetition and Transformation

* **C4.3.01｜Literal repetition**: Used for recognition, participation, ritual, and hooks; it is not automatically laziness.
* **C4.3.02｜Changed ending**: Retain the identity of the first part but change only the arrival to create continuation, a turn, or refusal.
* **C4.3.03｜Rhythmic transformation**: Retain interval or contour while changing duration, accent, syncopation, and rests.
* **C4.3.04｜Pitch transformation**: Retain rhythm or contour while transposing, inverting, expanding or contracting intervals, or changing mode.
* **C4.3.05｜Augmentation and diminution**: Change temporal scale so the same identity serves different structural levels.
* **C4.3.06｜Slicing**: Extract a fragment as developmental material while retaining cues to the original identity.
* **C4.3.07｜Inversion and retrograde**: Use only when aurally discernible or meaningful to a process, not as formal display.
* **C4.3.08｜Reharmonization**: When the melody remains and meaning changes, harmonic change must establish the new relationship clearly.
* **C4.3.09｜Reorchestration**: When material remains and role changes, timbre and register carry the narrative.
* **C4.3.10｜Role exchange**: A principal melody may become bass, inner voice, rhythm, or background texture.
* **C4.3.11｜Displacement**: Displacement across beats, bars, or phase must preserve a traceable anchor.
* **C4.3.12｜Combined reinterpretation**: Two old materials may appear together later to recover long-range causality.
* **C4.3.13｜Limit of change**: A transformation should not change every invariant at once unless extinction of identity is the purpose.

### C4.4 Structural Law: Transitions and Boundaries

* **C4.4.01｜Gradient transition**: Migrate gradually through density, register, harmony, filtering, or space.
* **C4.4.02｜Rupture transition**: Clearly announce a new state with a pause, cut, abrupt change, or reset.
* **C4.4.03｜Pivot transition**: Connect jurisdictions through a common tone, rhythm, timbre, or motive.
* **C4.4.04｜Foreshadowing**: Important material from a new section may first appear as background, fragments, or color.
* **C4.4.05｜Trailing-sound pull**: The last event of the preceding section may cause the first event of the next.
* **C4.4.06｜Do not overload boundaries**: A sectional boundary need not change every dimension at once unless a violent break of eras is intended.
* **C4.4.07｜False boundary**: A structure may appear to end and then continue, but must later recover the deceived expectation.
* **C4.4.08｜Transition duration**: Transition length is determined by the magnitude of change and the audience’s adaptation cost, not a fixed bar count.

### C4.5 Structural Law: Tension, Climax, and Ending

* **C4.5.01｜Sources of tension**: Tension may arise from unresolved harmony, rhythmic conflict, density, register, timbre, meaning, and memory.
* **C4.5.02｜Direction of tension**: Tension must be known to rise, hold, transfer, deceptively resolve, or collapse.
* **C4.5.03｜Asynchronous curves**: Harmonic tension may rise while loudness falls; different curves need not synchronize.
* **C4.5.04｜Graded peaks**: Local, sectional, and whole-work peaks must have a clear hierarchy.
* **C4.5.05｜Scarce extremes**: The unique highest pitch, widest range, densest texture, or fullest chord may act as a constitutional landmark.
* **C4.5.06｜Convergence before climax**: Before a climax, deletion, pauses, or repetition may narrow choices and increase inevitability.
* **C4.5.07｜Change after climax**: After the climax, change at least one of the world, theme, or mode of hearing.
* **C4.5.08｜False climax**: If there is a false climax, the real climax must surpass it in meaning, not mere loudness.
* **C4.5.09｜Closed ending**: Confirm belonging jointly through center, beat, syntax, and space.
* **C4.5.10｜Open ending**: Retain something explicitly unresolved; this is not arbitrary truncation.
* **C4.5.11｜Decaying ending**: Gradually reduce information, energy, or visibility so disappearance becomes a process.
* **C4.5.12｜Ruptured ending**: An abrupt stop must make the impossibility of continuing itself the conclusion.
* **C4.5.13｜Looping ending**: The end must return to the beginning without sounding as though a completed work is forcibly replayed.

### C5.1 Grammatical Law: Motives and Themes

* **C5.1.01｜Minimum identity**: A motive should be short enough to vary, clear enough to recognize, and open enough to develop.
* **C5.1.02｜One core first**: A short work should first be governed by one principal core, with secondary cores used for answer, conflict, or contrast.
* **C5.1.03｜Division by difference**: Multiple themes must be distinguishable in contour, rhythm, register, timbre, or function.
* **C5.1.04｜Rhythmic signature**: When pitch identity alone is insufficient, give the theme a stable fingerprint of duration and accent.
* **C5.1.05｜Intervallic signature**: Retaining one or two key intervals can let a theme survive across keys and orchestrations.
* **C5.1.06｜Timbral signature**: A particular articulation or texture may be part of a theme, but must permit necessary translation.
* **C5.1.07｜Leave room to develop**: A theme should not exhaust its highest pitch, full cadence, and complete orchestration on first appearance.
* **C5.1.08｜Answers have cause**: An answering theme should resolve, refute, amplify, or misunderstand the principal theme.
* **C5.1.09｜Effective fragments**: A thematic fragment must retain enough identity while remaining suitable for development and layering.
* **C5.1.10｜The theme’s destination**: The theme’s final appearance should reveal its fate rather than be an ordinary replay.

### C5.2 Grammatical Law: Melody

* **C5.2.01｜Competing accents**: Long notes, high notes, leaps, strong beats, and non-chord tones all compete for melodic accent.
* **C5.2.02｜Contour forms the phrase**: Rising, falling, arch, wave, and stasis should form a summarizable phrase shape.
* **C5.2.03｜Gradually opening range**: New registers should be earned as structure unfolds; avoid exposing the entire range at the opening.
* **C5.2.04｜Ordered peaks**: A local phrase peak must not unintentionally exceed the predetermined peak of the whole work.
* **C5.2.05｜Clear arrival**: A phrase’s final note must express completion, continuation, suspension, or questioning.
* **C5.2.06｜Directed non-chord tones**: Passing tones, neighbors, suspensions, appoggiaturas, and anticipations must have a logic of entry and departure.
* **C5.2.07｜Repeated notes have character**: Repetition of one pitch may express insistence, mechanics, declaration, anxiety, or rhythm; it must not stagnate without purpose.
* **C5.2.08｜Scarce large leaps**: Bind large leaps first to identity, emotional turns, textual keywords, or structural peaks.
* **C5.2.09｜Balance after leaps**: Unless successive large leaps are stylistically central, recover singability with steps, repetition, or rests.
* **C5.2.10｜Remember rhythm first**: A catchy melody often depends on both duration and contour; check rhythmic identity before changing pitch.
* **C5.2.11｜Breathing room in rests**: A melody must leave space for listener prediction, echoes, and accompanimental responses.
* **C5.2.12｜Variable phrase length**: Symmetrical phrases aid learning; asymmetrical phrases create advance, speech-like quality, and unease.
* **C5.2.13｜Modal scale**: Characteristic tones must appear at key positions and gain context, not merely run through the scale mechanically.
* **C5.2.14｜Ornament is subordinate**: Figuration, grace notes, and slides must not mask the principal contour.
* **C5.2.15｜Melody and bass**: They may reinforce in similar motion, expand in contrary motion, or create tension through displacement; the relationship must be clear.

### C5.3 Grammatical Law: Rhythm and Groove

* **C5.3.01｜Meter has meaning**: Meter serves accent organization and phrasing, not just notational convenience.
* **C5.3.02｜Stable subdivision**: The principal subdivision must be stable enough for swing, syncopation, and cross-rhythm to be perceived.
* **C5.3.03｜Division of accent**: Dynamic, pitch, duration, timbral, and harmonic accents may align or intentionally conflict.
* **C5.3.04｜Grades of syncopation**: Distinguish ornamental, sustained, structural, and cross-bar syncopation.
* **C5.3.05｜Cross-bar patterns can return**: A cross-bar pattern must periodically realign, be anchored by another layer, or turn misalignment itself into a process.
* **C5.3.06｜Tempo is not density**: Raising BPM, shortening note values, and increasing attack rate are different actions.
* **C5.3.07｜Independent harmonic rhythm**: Design the rate of chord change independently from drum density.
* **C5.3.08｜Rhythmic dialogue**: Parts may establish dialogue by filling gaps, echoing, anticipating beats, and delaying.
* **C5.3.09｜Holes in the low end**: Kick, bass, and sustained low tones must leave mutually readable temporal space.
* **C5.3.10｜Threshold for fills**: A fill serves boundaries, preparation, correction, or characterization; it is not forced in at a fixed interval.
* **C5.3.11｜Coherent swing**: Swing, shuffle, laid-back placement, and pushing ahead must be unified by part roles or intentionally differentiated.
* **C5.3.12｜Tempo change**: Accelerando and ritardando should change structural direction, not act as cheap emotional knobs.
* **C5.3.13｜Pauses define shape**: A pause must be long enough to change expectation; too short sounds mistaken, too long may create a new section.
* **C5.3.14｜Rhythmic memory**: A core rhythm may return across pitch, timbre, and parts.
* **C5.3.15｜Restrained complexity**: Difficulty should concentrate in a few layers while the others provide bodily or cognitive anchors.

### C5.4 Grammatical Law: Harmony and Tonality

* **C5.4.01｜Declare the center**: Tonality, modality, axis, pedal, or a centerless state must give listeners a usable reference.
* **C5.4.02｜Separate function and color**: A chord may provide direction or only color; do not confuse their evaluation.
* **C5.4.03｜Review the bass first**: First hear whether the bass line works, then discuss chordal complexity.
* **C5.4.04｜Harmonic rhythm**: Stable passages may change slowly and advancing passages may accelerate; the rate of change must serve structure.
* **C5.4.05｜Govern common tones**: Common tones may create continuity; moving inner voices may create implicit direction.
* **C5.4.06｜Directed chromatic lines**: Chromatic inner voices should be heard as tendency, friction, or color, not piled up without purpose.
* **C5.4.07｜Open low register**: Chords in the low register should normally be spaced more widely unless muddiness and beating are intended.
* **C5.4.08｜Guard against sharpness up high**: When dense high notes and bright timbres layer, review harshness and fatigue.
* **C5.4.09｜Dissonance has a function**: Dissonance may prepare, suspend, defer, tear, or become normal, but must belong to syntax.
* **C5.4.10｜Allocation of dominant-function authority**: Strong dominant function, substitute dominants, borrowed chords, and modal mixture must be used sparingly according to the authority they claim over resolution.
* **C5.4.11｜Modulation has a bridge**: Modulation must gain legitimacy through a pivot chord, common tone, melodic direction, bass, or rupture.
* **C5.4.12｜Pedals exert pressure**: A sustained bass must create perceptible friction or stability with change above, not merely serve convenience.
* **C5.4.13｜Graded cadence**: Authentic, plagal, half, deceptive, and open cadences carry different degrees of closure.
* **C5.4.14｜Audible extensions**: Ninths, elevenths, thirteenths, and other extensions must be truly perceptible in the voicing, not exist only in the label.
* **C5.4.15｜Reharmonization protects the principal line**: Reharmonization must not damage the meaning of key melody notes unless reinterpretation is the purpose.

### C5.5 Grammatical Law: Counterpoint, Voices, and Texture

* **C5.5.01｜Evidence of voice independence**: An independent voice must differ in at least one of rhythm, contour, register, or timbre.
* **C5.5.02｜Staggered activity**: Multiple voices should not all be highly active together; focus may rotate.
* **C5.5.03｜Use similar motion cautiously**: If all voices share rhythm and direction for long, polyphony disappears; state the meaning of their unison.
* **C5.5.04｜Contrary motion expands**: Contrary motion between melody and bass often enlarges space and may be a default check, not a rigid law.
* **C5.5.05｜Legitimacy of parallels**: Judge parallel intervals by style; traditional prohibitions may not overreach into modern jurisdictions.
* **C5.5.06｜Accountability for crossing**: Voice crossing must serve role exchange, approach, oppression, or a special timbre.
* **C5.5.07｜Inner voices have functions**: Inner voices should connect, create friction, add color, or supply rhythm, not fill chords unconsciously.
* **C5.5.08｜Restrained countermelody**: A countermelody must enter in gaps in the principal melody, repeated sections, or positions of low focus.
* **C5.5.09｜Heterogeneous textures**: Unison, chordal, polyphonic, arpeggiated, sustained, and pointillistic textures may serve as structural states.
* **C5.5.10｜Textural transformation**: Textural change should synchronize with sectional function, emotion, or a narrative turn.
* **C5.5.11｜Death of a voice**: A voice that no longer serves a function should exit rather than continue playing from project inertia.
* **C5.5.12｜Scarce convergence**: Complete convergence of multiple independent voices may become a landmark of climax, consensus, or loss of individuality.

### C5.6 Grammatical Law: Lyrics, Speech, and Voice

* **C5.6.01｜Syllabic correspondence**: Choose syllabic, sustained, or melismatic setting according to linguistic clarity and style.
* **C5.6.02｜Stress correspondence**: Keywords take priority on strong beats, long notes, phrase peaks, or points of harmonic change.
* **C5.6.03｜Vowel fit**: Favor sustainable vowels on high and long notes, avoiding physiological conflict in articulation.
* **C5.6.04｜Consonant clarity**: Dense accompaniment and long reverb must not swallow initial consonants and semantic boundaries.
* **C5.6.05｜Curve of tone**: Questions, exclamations, statements, irony, and hesitation must have counterparts in melody and rhythm.
* **C5.6.06｜Narrative viewpoint**: Switching among first person, observer, group, and character should change sonic position and orchestral relationships.
* **C5.6.07｜Rhyme does not overrule**: Rhyme and word count must not sacrifice natural word order, persona, or musical arrival.
* **C5.6.08｜Chorus fulfillment**: A chorus should answer the core question more clearly than a verse in meaning, range, rhythm, or harmony.
* **C5.6.09｜Changed repetition**: Repeated lyrics may acquire new meaning through changes in singing, harmony, orchestration, and context.
* **C5.6.10｜Wordless voice**: Humming, chanting, and the voice as sound still require a timbral role and syntax.

### C5.7 Grammatical Law: Silence and Negative Space

* **C5.7.01｜Classes of silence**: Distinguish breathing, waiting, severance, shock, resonance, and spatial silence.
* **C5.7.02｜Ownership of empty space**: Negative space should clearly be left for listener prediction, another part’s response, or sonic decay.
* **C5.7.03｜Length of silence**: Length must fit its level; a gap between notes and a sectional rupture are not interchangeable.
* **C5.7.04｜History before silence**: The more forceful a pause should be, the more it needs prior stable motion or high density as a norm.
* **C5.7.05｜Consequences of silence**: The first event after a pause should acknowledge the reset that the pause produced.

### C6.1 Orchestration Law: Roles and Ensemble

* **C6.1.01｜Role before instrument**: Define the function first, then choose the instrument and register best able to perform it.
* **C6.1.02｜Roles can change**: One instrument may change roles and one role may change instruments, but the transition must be audible.
* **C6.1.03｜One principal narrator**: If multiple principal narrators exist at once, they must form dialogue or polyphony, not overlap accidentally.
* **C6.1.04｜Subtractive support**: A supporting part should first fill what is missing rather than copy existing information.
* **C6.1.05｜Counterpose through difference**: A counterposing part should differ effectively from the protagonist in rhythm, register, timbre, or direction.
* **C6.1.06｜Perceptible resistance**: A part carrying resistance should create explicit friction and be handled within the structure.
* **C6.1.07｜Echo has distance**: An echo must show in time, space, timbre, or completeness that it is not the original sound.
* **C6.1.08｜Environment does not seize authority**: An environmental layer establishes world and continuity and should not unintentionally become the principal melody.
* **C6.1.09｜Increasing the ensemble**: A new instrument must add a new role, scale, or color, not merely loudness.
* **C6.1.10｜Reducing the ensemble**: Removal can expose the core, create fragility, clear space, or prepare a return.

### C6.2 Orchestration Law: Timbre, Register, and Sound Production

* **C6.2.01｜Dimensions of timbre**: Timbre must be decomposed into at least brightness, roughness, harmonicity, noise content, attack, sustain, and dynamic response.
* **C6.2.02｜Personality of register**: Treat different registers of the same instrument as different roles, not as one uniform timbre.
* **C6.2.03｜Layering attacks**: When several parts with strong attacks appear together, stagger them, separate their bands, or change foreground and background.
* **C6.2.04｜Govern sustain**: Sustained parts must leave temporal and spectral windows for brief information.
* **C6.2.05｜First appearance of timbre**: A new timbre should carry familiar material on first appearance, reducing double novelty.
* **C6.2.06｜First appearance of material**: New material should be carried by a familiar timbre on first appearance, protecting recognition.
* **C6.2.07｜Blended color has a core**: Layered timbres should produce one discernible composite identity, not a mutually masking stack.
* **C6.2.08｜Reserve extremes**: The brightest, darkest, roughest, and purest timbral states should be reserved for structural nodes.
* **C6.2.09｜Layer distortion**: Saturation, overdrive, bit-crush, and noise must be controlled by frequency band, part, and time span.
* **C6.2.10｜Timbral memory**: When the same timbre returns later, changes in space, register, or articulation may give it new meaning.

### C6.3 Orchestration Law: Instrumental Nature and Playability

* **C6.3.01｜Comfortable range**: Place normal material first in an instrument’s stable range and use extreme registers for specific effects.
* **C6.3.02｜Breathing and bowing**: Long lines must allow breathing, bow changes, position changes, pedaling, and bodily reset.
* **C6.3.03｜Speed limit**: Fast figures must fit the realities of the instrument’s articulation, fingering, and register.
* **C6.3.04｜Polyphonic capability**: Chord count, span, and sustain must fit the instrument’s physics and performance tradition.
* **C6.3.05｜Authentic articulation**: Legato, staccato, tenuto, and accents must match what the sound source and instrument can realize.
* **C6.3.06｜Native ornament**: Trills, slides, vibrato, harmonics, and hammer-ons should use the instrument’s own vocabulary.
* **C6.3.07｜Even virtual instruments keep a body**: A virtual instrument may exceed humans, but the excess must be declared as a surreal aesthetic.
* **C6.3.08｜Dynamic response**: MIDI velocity, expression control, and timbral layer switching must reflect the instrument’s real or deliberately targeted response.
* **C6.3.09｜Sample alternation**: Repeated notes should manage round-robin, legato, and retriggering to avoid the machine-gun effect unless it is wanted.
* **C6.3.10｜Performer review**: Highly difficult or culturally specific writing should be reviewed by someone with corresponding performance experience.

### C6.4 Performance Law: Timing, Dynamics, and Expression

* **C6.4.01｜Shaping within the beat**: Microtiming should form pushing, leaning back, suspension, or breathing around beat functions.
* **C6.4.02｜Phrase-end flexibility**: Lengthening or shortening at phrase ends must fit the boundary level and style.
* **C6.4.03｜Dynamic arcs**: Dynamics should form direction across motives and phrases, not randomize every note independently.
* **C6.4.04｜Graded accents**: Structural, linguistic, metric, and emotional accents may combine or separate.
* **C6.4.05｜Control continuity**: Legato is not merely overlapping notes; clarity created by note changes, attacks, and pedaling must be managed.
* **C6.4.06｜Short notes have tails**: The duration and release of short articulation determine weight, speed, and space, not merely note-off.
* **C6.4.07｜Vibrato in time**: The entrance, speed, width, and exit of vibrato should change with syntax.
* **C6.4.08｜Layered pedaling**: Piano pedaling must handle harmonic changes, low-frequency accumulation, resonance, and clarity.
* **C6.4.09｜Microvariation in repetition**: A repeated phrase may express new history through touch, breath, dynamics, or timing.
* **C6.4.10｜Do not overperform emotion**: Performance intensity must not spend the climax before structural intensity arrives.

### C6.5 Spatial Law: Distance, Direction, and World

* **C6.5.01｜Meaning of distance**: Proximity serves intimacy, clarity, and embodiment; distance serves memory, environment, grandeur, or inaccessibility.
* **C6.5.02｜Division of width**: Core low frequencies and central information normally remain stable; width serves enclosure, expansion, or separation.
* **C6.5.03｜Layered reverb**: Early reflections determine distance and spatial shape; the tail determines resonance and world scale.
* **C6.5.04｜Changing dry/wet balance**: Space may approach, recede, collapse, or open with sections and need not remain fixed throughout.
* **C6.5.05｜Pre-delay**: Pre-delay may protect the protagonist’s attack while retaining a large space.
* **C6.5.06｜Constrain low frequencies**: Low-frequency spatial diffusion must be reviewed for phase, localization, and system translation.
* **C6.5.07｜Dramatic panning**: Movement and placement should correspond to role, action, viewpoint, or structure, not wander without cause.
* **C6.5.08｜Spatial negative space**: Spatial effects also consume attention and spectrum; reverb tails must be included in orchestration.

### C7.1 Production Law: Spectrum and Masking

* **C7.1.01｜Judge in the full mix**: Judge spectral processing first in the full mix; beauty in solo must not substitute for correct relationships.
* **C7.1.02｜Locate the problem**: First identify who conflicts, when, and in which frequency band, then choose static or dynamic treatment.
* **C7.1.03｜Subtraction first**: For nonfunctional overlap, first reduce arrangement, register, and duration, then remedy with EQ.
* **C7.1.04｜High-pass with cause**: High-pass filtering removes useless energy; do not apply fixed cutoff points mechanically by track type.
* **C7.1.05｜Review low mids**: Thickness and muddiness often coexist in the low mids; reduction must preserve embodiment.
* **C7.1.06｜Restrain presence**: Upper-mid boosts must prevent the protagonist, snare, guitars, and bright synths from becoming harsh together.
* **C7.1.07｜Dynamic yielding**: Only while conflict occurs, use dynamic EQ, multiband processing, or sidechaining for temporary yielding.
* **C7.1.08｜Harmonic compensation**: When a small system cannot reproduce a low fundamental, use controlled harmonics to improve audibility rather than only adding bass.
* **C7.1.09｜Change the bus cautiously**: Bus EQ must not conceal problems in individual tracks or arrangement.
* **C7.1.10｜Reference matching**: Reference tracks calibrate spectrum and scale; they are not used to copy an entire curve.

### C7.2 Production Law: Dynamics, Transients, and Density

* **C7.2.01｜Layered dynamics**: Distinguish note, part, sectional, and master loudness dynamics.
* **C7.2.02｜Compression has a function**: Compression must explicitly control peaks, shape envelopes, increase density, glue, or create pumping.
* **C7.2.03｜Preserve attacks**: Compressor attack and release must protect the target transient and groove.
* **C7.2.04｜Accountability for overcompression**: Loudness gained by losing breath, hierarchy, and sectional difference is unlawful.
* **C7.2.05｜Transient shaping**: Strengthening or weakening attacks should change role, not uniformly make everything “punchier.”
* **C7.2.06｜Manage sustain**: Shortening sustain can clear space; lengthening it can enlarge weight and continuity.
* **C7.2.07｜Disciplined sidechaining**: Sidechain amount, curve, and release must serve low-frequency relationships and style, not merely seek pumping.
* **C7.2.08｜Graded saturation**: First distinguish mild density, stylistic coloration, and destructive distortion, then choose algorithm and intensity.
* **C7.2.09｜Parallel processing has a core**: Parallel compression or distortion must preserve the original dynamic core and avoid double blurring.
* **C7.2.10｜Density is not loudness**: Evaluate layer count, spectral occupancy, attack rate, and the sensation of compression separately.

### C7.3 Production Law: Mix Hierarchy and Space

* **C7.3.01｜Faders first**: Establish hierarchy first with volume, panning, muting, and arrangement, then open complex plug-ins.
* **C7.3.02｜Automate the protagonist**: Subtle automation may keep the protagonist readable; it need not remain at a fixed louder level throughout.
* **C7.3.03｜Background can recede**: When primary information appears, the background should yield automatically or through arrangement.
* **C7.3.04｜Rhythm of reverb**: Reverb tails must be compatible with tempo, harmonic changes, and syntactic boundaries.
* **C7.3.05｜Delay has a place**: Delay should fill gaps, create space, or establish memory without covering subsequent statements.
* **C7.3.06｜Stable center**: Keep the placement of lead vocal, principal melody, low frequencies, and core drum strikes stable for the target medium.
* **C7.3.07｜Mono review**: After width processing, check phase, low frequencies, and core identity in mono.
* **C7.3.08｜Narrative automation**: Automation of filtering, space, width, and distortion should correspond to structural curves.
* **C7.3.09｜Govern noise**: Noise floor, ambience, and texture must have stable hierarchy and must not consume detail.
* **C7.3.10｜Review the print**: After offline rendering, recheck tails, automation, sample switching, and randomness.

### C7.4 Production Law: Mastering, Release, and Technical Quality Control

* **C7.4.01｜Mastering does not rescue composition**: Mastering unifies, translates, and finalizes; it is not responsible for repairing structure or orchestration.
* **C7.4.02｜Loudness fits the medium**: Choose target loudness according to platform, style, and dynamic need; do not pursue one universal LUFS value.
* **C7.4.03｜True-peak safety**: Check true peaks and clipping after limiting, encoding, and sample-rate conversion.
* **C7.4.04｜Dynamic range**: LRA and short-term dynamics must fit emotion, situation, and the audience’s environment.
* **C7.4.05｜Encoding review**: After lossy encoding, check high frequencies, transients, stereo, and low-level textures.
* **C7.4.06｜Device matrix**: Review on at least the main monitors, ordinary headphones, small speakers, and the target endpoint.
* **C7.4.07｜Volume matrix**: Check hierarchy at low volume, harshness and fatigue at high volume, and the whole at medium volume.
* **C7.4.08｜Complete head and tail**: Check initial silence, fade-in, final resonance, loop points, and metadata.
* **C7.4.09｜Traceable versions**: Retain version, parameters, random seed, and change notes for every render.
* **C7.4.10｜Reproducibility**: The same input should reconstruct the same core result; random variation must have a seed or range.

### C8.1 Review Law: Evaluation Vectors

* **C8.1.01｜Audibility**: Are the protagonist, hierarchy, boundaries, and low-frequency relationships clear?
* **C8.1.02｜Identity**: Can the work be recognized by one or two irreplaceable features?
* **C8.1.03｜Learnability**: Can listeners form an internal model of motives, beat, gravity, and form?
* **C8.1.04｜Effective surprise**: Does a deviation have context, magnitude, and consequences?
* **C8.1.05｜Structural causality**: Are sections and climaxes earned by what precedes them?
* **C8.1.06｜Emotional fit**: Does the actual listening impression match the target vector rather than merely the verbal label?
* **C8.1.07｜Stylistic credibility**: Does the work use the relationships of the relevant jurisdiction rather than surface symbols?
* **C8.1.08｜Vitality**: Do performance and timbre possess direction, embodiment, and role?
* **C8.1.09｜Repeat-listening value**: After identity has been learned, do long-range relationships, microdifferences, and reinterpretations remain?
* **C8.1.10｜Irreplaceability**: If melody, timbre, climax position, or harmony is replaced, does the work clearly lose itself?
* **C8.1.11｜Technical stability**: Are unintended clipping, masking, artifacts, truncated tails, and failures across devices controlled?
* **C8.1.12｜Fitness for use**: Does the work succeed in its target medium, duration, and audience environment?

### C8.2 Review Law: Listening-Test Procedure

* **C8.2.01｜Difference before preference**: First confirm that listeners can consistently hear differences between versions, then discuss which is better.
* **C8.2.02｜Loudness matching**: A/B, ABX, and multi-version comparisons must first be perceptually loudness-matched.
* **C8.2.03｜Randomized order**: Randomize playback order to prevent primacy, recency, and familiarity biases.
* **C8.2.04｜Complete excerpts**: A 15-to-30-second test excerpt should contain enough context and not isolate a highlight.
* **C8.2.05｜Listen at multiple scales**: Review one-second, ten-second, sectional, and whole-work scales separately.
* **C8.2.06｜Separate questions**: Ask separately about liking, clarity, catchiness, emotion, style, desire to relisten, and technical quality.
* **C8.2.07｜Record reasons**: After scoring, require identification of specific moments and reasons to reduce empty numbers.
* **C8.2.08｜Record familiarity**: Record participants’ familiarity with the style, instruments, and work.
* **C8.2.09｜Record equipment**: Record headphones, speakers, volume, and environment to avoid treating playback differences as differences in the work.
* **C8.2.10｜Repeat listening in rounds**: First listening tests accessibility; repeated listening tests learning and durability; do not conflate them in one round.

### C8.3 Review Law: Failure Diagnosis

* **C8.3.01｜Unclear**: Check roles, register, timing, and masking before EQ.
* **C8.3.02｜Unmemorable**: Check the identity core, contour, rhythm, and repetition points before asking whether the notes are beautiful.
* **C8.3.03｜Too flat**: Check the direction of tension, scarce landmarks, role changes, and long-range recovery before blindly adding layers.
* **C8.3.04｜Too chaotic**: Check simultaneous novelty, focus, stable anchors, and hierarchy before simplifying everything globally.
* **C8.3.05｜Unsatisfying climax**: Check prior history, scarcity, recovery, and change after the climax, not only loudness.
* **C8.3.06｜Emotion goes astray**: Examine valence, arousal, agency, motion, closure, and space separately to find the conflicting dimension.
* **C8.3.07｜Sounds like a template**: Check irreplaceable identity, prohibited shortcuts, style mixture, and distinctive precedents.
* **C8.3.08｜Sounds like collage**: Check division among jurisdictions, shared invariants, transitional pivots, and overall sovereignty.
* **C8.3.09｜Mechanically rigid**: Check syntactic timing, dynamics, articulation, and instrumental embodiment; do not sprinkle random humanization.
* **C8.3.10｜Each revision gets worse**: Return to the last version with a definite improvement and change only one principal conflict per round.

### C8.4 Review Law: Precedent, Versions, and Amendment

* **C8.4.01｜Precedent format**: Record context, conflicting clauses, candidates, choice, auditory result, applicable jurisdictions, and confidence.
* **C8.4.02｜One case does not legislate**: One success enters the case library only; it does not directly become a universal rule.
* **C8.4.03｜Repetition becomes practice**: After repeated reproduction in similar contexts, a stylistic practice may form.
* **C8.4.04｜Cross-jurisdiction validity becomes law**: A practice may rise to a universal clause only when it remains valid across works and jurisdictions.
* **C8.4.05｜Preserve counterexamples**: Important counterexamples must be preserved with the rule to prevent later models from overgeneralizing.
* **C8.4.06｜Version semantics**: Every version states what it added, removed, sacrificed, and protected.
* **C8.4.07｜Regression testing**: After revising a rule, retest with old works, different styles, and extreme emotions.
* **C8.4.08｜Conditions for repeal**: A rule should be downgraded or repealed if it remains nonpredictive, is gamed through metrics, or obstructs the objective.
* **C8.4.09｜Constitutional freeze**: Freeze high-level clauses within one project cycle to prevent standards from drifting during composition.
* **C8.4.10｜Quarterly revision**: Review precedents centrally before revising in batches; do not amend instantly with each change of mood.

### C8.5 Agent Execution Law: From Words to Sound

* **C8.5.01｜Parse**: First decompose natural language into sovereignty, emotion vector, motion vector, jurisdictions, and prohibited shortcuts.
* **C8.5.02｜Constitute**: Generate a C2 Work-Specific Charter and mark uncertainties and contestable choices.
* **C8.5.03｜Multiple proposals**: Generate at least three schemes with different tradeoffs, not parameter jitter around one scheme.
* **C8.5.04｜Compose by layers**: Realize the whole work → sections → phrases → events level by level; do not write straight through from the first note to the last.
* **C8.5.05｜Conflict report**: For every choice, state which clause was protected, which was sacrificed, and why the proportion is reasonable.
* **C8.5.06｜Separate rendering**: Review symbolic composition, orchestration, performance, and mixing in stages so later layers do not conceal earlier ones.
* **C8.5.07｜Automated quality control**: Check range, collisions, clipping, masking, thematic recurrence, counts of extremes, and curve deviation.
* **C8.5.08｜Auditory loop**: Automated metrics only filter errors; final candidates must undergo real rendering and listening tests.
* **C8.5.09｜Write back cases**: Write effective revisions to the precedent library rather than directly changing all preferences of the generative model.
* **C8.5.10｜Preserve personality**: An Agent should provide judgments and choices, not average the work into the training set’s safe center.

## 5. Amendment Discipline

* One success does not legislate; one failure does not repeal.
* A local precedent may form only after the same kind of result appears in at least three independent works.
* It may rise to stylistic law only if it remains valid across authors, orchestrations, or audiences.
* Amendment of C1 may be discussed only when multiple stylistic laws expose the same fundamental conflict.
* C0 is amended only when the system’s objective, interpretive authority, or conflict mechanism fails.
* Every amendment retains a version number, counterexamples, rollback point, and scope of applicability.

---

**Principle of enactment**: There are many clauses, but each does only one thing; the hierarchy is strict, but does not lock down local answers; transgression is allowed, but consequence-free transgression is not.
