>
> **Version boundary**: v0.2 replaces v0.1 as the official constitution for new
> workflows. Existing works and workflows are not retroactively judged wrong
> because this version exists; v0.1 remains available in Git history.

> **Nature**: Non-normative musical principles for human creators, general-purpose
> AI, and the Tianlai creative workflow.
>
> **It is not responsible for proving why a work is correct.**
>
> The Workflow preserves facts, traces relations of consequence, constrains
> revisions, reviews the whole work, and preserves branches. This Constitution

>
> **Being explainable does not make something valid; being temporarily
> inexplicable does not make it worthless.**
>
> **Every judgment must ultimately return to sound.**

## 0. How to Use This Constitution

This Constitution no longer attempts to be a complete manual of music theory,
orchestration, mixing, and production.

Traditional harmony, melodic writing, style recipes, instrumental ranges,
technical quality control, loudness, spectrum, and file contracts may each have
their own reference manuals, precedents, and machine checks. Unless they concern
creative freedom, the identity of a work, or aesthetic judgment, they should not
be elevated into constitutional law.

The Constitution specifies **what has the right to exist during creation, what
must not be silently substituted, and what deserves to be questioned again**.

### C0｜Metaprinciples

* **C0.01｜Creative Space**: The Constitution protects possibility first; it does not prove its worth by reducing the range of possibilities.
* **C0.02｜Separation of Procedural Powers**: Factual consistency, revision scope, causal fences, version relations, and evidence contracts belong to the Workflow; the Constitution must not create a second, duplicate procedure.
* **C0.03｜Rules Must Step Aside**: A question that can be determined by the engine, Schema, instrument roster, or technical inspection must not be elevated into aesthetic law.
* **C0.04｜The Ear Has Final Authority**: Words, theory, metrics, derivations, and clauses provide ways of observing; stable, reproducible audible differences have final authority.
* **C0.05｜Reasons Are Not Evidence**: The fact that a choice can be explained elegantly is not evidence of its musical value.
* **C0.06｜The Right to Remain Unspoken**: Material may appear before its reason; it must not be deleted merely because the creator cannot yet explain its function.
* **C0.07｜Retrospective Validity**: Material that began as chance, intuition, or experiment may earn its place through relationships formed later.
* **C0.08｜Do Not Reverse Causality**: Meaning formed later may change how earlier material is understood, but it must not be falsely presented as that material's true motivation from the beginning.
* **C0.09｜Multiple Answers Are Legitimate**: One question may have multiple complete answers that cannot fully replace one another.
* **C0.10｜Protection of the Unknown**: A sonic relationship is not inferior because no established name, theoretical category, or historical precedent exists for it.
* **C0.11｜Culture Is Context-Bound**: Every judgment of style, grammar, and aesthetics belongs to a cultural, historical, medial, and audience context; none may pretend to be an unconditional universal law.
* **C0.12｜Technical Honesty**: Unintentional clipping, resource errors, incorrect ranges, truncated tails, and other technical failures must not evade scrutiny in the name of aesthetics; an intentional transgression must explicitly become part of the work.
* **C0.13｜Exceptions Always Exist**: The work itself may overturn any lower-order principle; if the work is clearly more convincing after doing so, the principle must yield.
* **C0.14｜Against Averaging**: Distinctive choices must not be averaged into a safe, correct, and impersonal middle merely to satisfy more clauses.

# C1｜A Work Taking Shape and a World Coming Alive

Music can be “good” in at least two different senses.

In one, the work's underlying form has taken shape: its material is recognizable,
its temporal relationships are credible, its structure can carry memory, and its
melody, rhythm, harmony, or other identity can sustain further development.

In the other, it has truly formed a sonic world one can enter.

The former is called **Chengqu: the work taking shape**.

The latter is called **Chengjing: the world coming alive**.

Neither may impersonate the other.

### C1.1｜Two Forms of Coherence

* **C1.1.01｜Melody Is Not the Whole Work**: A good melody does not automatically produce good music.
* **C1.1.02｜The Whole Is Not the Sum of Its Melodies**: A piece of music as a whole may possess qualities that no individual part possesses.
* **C1.1.03｜Chengqu Review**: A work should make its identity, temporal direction, and principal relationships perceptible rather than relying entirely on lavish rendering to conceal emptiness.
* **C1.1.04｜Chengjing Review**: Once the underlying form holds, the sonic world must still be examined for flow, layers, breath, distance, texture, and life.
* **C1.1.05｜The Bare-Melody Test Is Not Universal**: Singing or playing a melody without accompaniment carries great weight only when melody is genuinely the work's principal identity.
* **C1.1.06｜Equal Standing for Non-Melodic Works**: A work centered on groove, timbre, texture, space, polyphony, process, or sonic behavior is not inferior because it lacks a prominent principal melody.
* **C1.1.07｜Primacy Is Not Subordination**: Greater attention to a principal voice does not mean that every other part must derive its meaning from that voice.
* **C1.1.08｜Simple Underlying Form Is Legitimate**: An extremely simple melody, harmony, or rhythm may sustain an exceptionally rich musical world.
* **C1.1.09｜Complex Underlying Form Is Legitimate**: Complex material may also be valid, but complexity does not automatically confer depth.
* **C1.1.10｜The Whole Can Emerge**: If a detail sounds ordinary in isolation but creates an irreplaceable effect within the full work, it should be judged by that whole-work relationship.
* **C1.1.11｜What Cannot Stand Alone May Still Be Necessary**: A part need not succeed as a solo in order to deserve a place as an indispensable part of the whole.
* **C1.1.12｜Sparseness Is Also a Choice**: If a work draws its force from exposure, silence, or a single line, it must not be forced to add layers merely to become “richer.”

### C1.2｜Identity

* **C1.2.01｜Identity May Have Many Sources**: A work's identity may arise from melody, rhythm, harmony, timbre, space, texture, process, performing gesture, or the relationships among them.
* **C1.2.02｜Identity Is Not a Label**: A work need not be fully summarized by a single style label.
* **C1.2.03｜The Center May Move**: A work may shift its actual center of attention at different stages, provided that the changes belong to the same history.
* **C1.2.04｜Multiple Centers Are Legitimate**: When several parts, processes, or sonic layers jointly constitute a work, no permanent ruler of the entire work need be chosen.
* **C1.2.05｜Identity May Remain Blurred**: Ambiguity, drift, or dual identity may itself become a stable feature of the work.

# C2｜Germination and Xiangyin

A work is not written only after all its reasons are already known.

Some things happen first.

Some things are repeated.

Some things meet material that appears later and, for the first time, acquire
real weight.

**Germination permits the absence of reasons; once Xiangyin begins, choices
gradually begin to carry consequences.**

### C2.1｜The Right to Germinate

* **C2.1.01｜The Seed Comes First**: Melody, rhythm, timbre, chord, noise, gesture, or any small relationship may enter a work before it has a complete explanation.
* **C2.1.02｜No Interrogation in Advance**: The generative stage does not require every note, every part, and every entrance to supply a verbal reason beforehand.
* **C2.1.03｜Intuition Is Legitimate**: Intuition, chance, accidental gestures, and experimental results may all become candidate material.
* **C2.1.04｜Chance Does Not Escape Review**: A chance occurrence earns admission as a candidate, not an automatic right to permanent retention.
* **C2.1.05｜Listen Before Naming**: When an effect cannot immediately be classified, listen first to its relationships and decide later whether it needs a name.
* **C2.1.06｜The Question May Arise Later**: A work may first produce material and only gradually discover what problem it is actually addressing.

### C2.2｜Xiangyin

* **C2.2.01｜Relationships Create Constraints**: Once material has been established, repeated, remembered, or brought into relationship with other material, it may become a genuine premise for later choices.
* **C2.2.02｜Necessity Is Context-Bound**: “It could not be otherwise” is always true only within a particular work, history, and set of premises; it makes no claim to universal truth.
* **C2.2.03｜Prior History Has Standing**: Major later choices should confront the material already established instead of treating the work as a blank page each time.
* **C2.2.04｜Alternatives Must Be Real**: An alternative used to demonstrate the necessity of a choice should have been a genuinely viable path at the time, not a weak straw alternative.
* **C2.2.05｜Explanation Must Not Fabricate Facts**: Emotional stories, symbolic meanings, and authorial statements must not substitute for relationships that actually exist in the score and sound.
* **C2.2.06｜Local Events Need No Excessive Proof**: A small event that does not wield authority over identity, structure, or a major turn must not be forced to bear causal proof disproportionate to its scale.
* **C2.2.07｜Structural Debt**: The more major, conspicuous, and irreversible an event is, the more it should carry prior history, consequences, or an explicit rupture.
* **C2.2.08｜Microscopic Freedom**: The closer an event lies to texture, breath, and peripheral perception, the more local autonomy it may possess.
* **C2.2.09｜New Relationships May Recast Old Material**: Later development may give earlier material a new way of being heard, but its original history of occurrence should be preserved.
* **C2.2.10｜Discovery May Flow Backward**: If a relationship discovered late is strong enough to alter structural judgment, it must leave the status of “refinement” and return to formal revision and Xiangyin.
* **C2.2.11｜The Unresolved Is Legitimate**: Not every question must be resolved in the current version; an unresolved question with genuine life may be preserved.
* **C2.2.12｜Do Not Iterate When Nothing Has Happened**: When there is no real conflict, new possibility, or audible problem, do not force another revision merely to display depth of thought.

# C3｜Attention and the Ecology of Parts

Human attention is not a spotlight capable of illuminating only one point.

A listener may know clearly what occupies the foreground while being affected
by many sounds that never become objects of conscious attention.

The principal voice, supporting voices, and background in music are therefore
not ranks of power, but different **positions in perception**.

### C3.1｜The Field of Attention

* **C3.1.01｜Focus May Be Singular or Multiple**: A single focus suits a great deal of music, but it is not a universal obligation.
* **C3.1.02｜Foreground, Middleground, and Background**: Foreground, middleground, and background describe perceptual salience, not degrees of value.
* **C3.1.03｜The Background May Live**: A background part may possess its own contour, rhythm, motion, and history; it need not be merely a sustained chord.
* **C3.1.04｜Accompaniment Is Not a Servant**: Accompaniment may support, counterpose, evade, proceed independently, breathe, or simply coexist; it need not explain the principal voice at every moment.
* **C3.1.05｜Companion Parts**: A part may work primarily in peripheral perception: attentive listening may reveal its own direction, while inattentive listening lets it merge into the overall breath.
* **C3.1.06｜Entrances and Exits May Be Light**: A part's entrance or departure need not always carry sectional, narrative, or energetic meaning; sustaining the vitality of the world may itself suffice.
* **C3.1.07｜Density Is Not Presumed Guilty**: Before adding a part, one need not first answer “what new information does it add?”; first determine whether it changes the overall listening experience.
* **C3.1.08｜Complexity May Run in Parallel**: Several simultaneously active layers are not inherently wrong, provided they can be perceived as meaningful fusion, stratification, or multiple streams.
* **C3.1.09｜Fusion Is Also Structure**: Several instruments may fuse into a composite color that is difficult to separate and may carry the identity of the work just as an independent melody can.
* **C3.1.10｜Passing Out of Step Is Legitimate**: Different parts need not always express the same emotion or direction at the same moment; slightly unsynchronized lives can make a sonic world more credible.
* **C3.1.11｜A Side Branch May Have a History**: Non-principal material may return, change, or disappear across sections without being promoted into a formal theme.
* **C3.1.12｜Seizing Attention Requires Promotion**: If an originally peripheral part repeatedly becomes a salient object of attention, its role should be acknowledged anew rather than pretending it remains mere background.

### C3.2｜Emptiness and Room

* **C3.2.01｜Empty Space Has Rights**: A place occupied by no part is not a defect.
* **C3.2.02｜Space Must Not Be Filled by Checklist**: An “empty” frequency band, beat position, or measure is not by itself a reason to add notes.
* **C3.2.03｜Not Responding Is Also a Response**: When the principal melody leaves a gap, another part may answer, or the gap may be allowed to remain.
* **C3.2.04｜Silence Belongs to Texture**: Rests, resonance, and waiting are part of the total texture, not merely absences between events.

# C4｜Qiyun and Chengjing

Once a work's principal material, relationships, and structure can already stand,
it may enter an independent process of **Chengjing**, in which its sonic world
comes alive.

This is not a matter of sprinkling decoration over a finished product.

It asks:

> **Where in this world does something merely “exist correctly” without yet
> truly coming alive?**

The object examined at this level is provisionally called **Qiyun**.

“Qiyun” is neither a fixed style of Chinese music nor a mystical metric. It is
simply an umbrella term for sonic relationships that are difficult to assign to
the principal melody yet genuinely alter the overall sense of life.

### C4.1｜The Right to Form a World

* **C4.1.01｜The Final Generative Layer**: Chengjing is the last active generative stage after the principal structure has taken shape, but it is not an irreversible final step.
* **C4.1.02｜Addition Is Not the Goal**: Chengjing may add notes, but it may also remove, delay, advance, lighten, distance, separate, or leave empty.
* **C4.1.03｜First Ask What Kind of Life Is Missing**: Do not begin with “what instrument can still be added?”; first ask what motion, air, distance, response, or aftertaste the whole currently lacks.
* **C4.1.04｜Embellishment Must Not Become a Template**: Do not mechanically insert fills, arpeggios, bells, harp glissandi, woodwind responses, or other tokens of “sophistication” at fixed metrical intervals.
* **C4.1.05｜Small Sounds Need No Grand Reason**: A tiny part may do nothing more than alter the breath; it need not be dressed up as a theme, symbol, or narrative event.
* **C4.1.06｜Proof by Deletion**: For companion material whose function cannot be clearly explained, prefer judging it through deletion or mute comparison rather than forcing language to manufacture a reason.
* **C4.1.07｜Loss Is Sufficient**: If removing material reliably diminishes space, flow, weight, warmth, tension, continuity, bodily presence, or the credibility of the world, that loss is itself evidence.
* **C4.1.08｜What Cannot Be Perceived Should Recede**: If repeated comparisons under matched conditions still do not make the material's presence or absence reliably perceptible, do not preserve its complexity by default.
* **C4.1.09｜The Periphery Comes First**: During Chengjing, first seek means that can alter the whole without demanding primary attention.
* **C4.1.10｜Repetition Has Weather**: When the same theme returns, the surrounding world may change subtly because of the history already lived; it need not reproduce the atmosphere of the first appearance.
* **C4.1.11｜Small Sounds May Be Incomplete**: A companion line may consist of only two or three notes, a single passing trace, one resonance, or a gesture that never finishes its reply.
* **C4.1.12｜Non-Melodic Means Have Equal Standing**: Qiyun may arise from timbre, envelope, dynamics, space, resonance, microtiming, texture, and silence; it need not continually add new melodies.
* **C4.1.13｜Differences Must Be Correlated**: A microdifference may lack semantics, but it must not degenerate into independent random jitter across unrelated parameters.
* **C4.1.14｜Do Not Over-Awaken**: If every background element remains active at all times, “aliveness” becomes agitation; stillness is also part of the ecology.
* **C4.1.15｜Step Back at the Structural Boundary**: If a Qiyun revision changes thematic identity, harmonic causality, sectional function, climax logic, or the work's stance, it is no longer refinement and must return to the formal revision process.
* **C4.1.16｜Zero Revision Is Legitimate**: A Chengjing review may conclude that nothing more should be added here.

### C4.2｜Common Forms of Qiyun

The following are not a required checklist, only types that are allowed to be heard:

* **Companion line**: An independent line with a small direction of its own that does not carry the principal statement.
* **Echo**: Not a complete repetition, but the distance, fragment, or memory left by a sound.
* **Micromotion**: Slow changes of pitch, timbre, dynamics, or texture within a sustained sonic field.
* **Point of light**: A sound that appears very rarely and changes the quality of only one instant.
* **Breath**: Temporal elasticity formed by entrances, exits, pauses, extensions, and contractions.
* **After-resonance**: Resonance and tail that continue to participate in the next moment after a note ends.
* **Shadow layer**: A sound that is scarcely noticed directly yet changes the contour, color, or spatial sense of the principal layer.
* **Passing out of step**: Several living streams remain part of the same world without being fully synchronized.

Any of these may be absent.

# C5｜Timbre, Instruments, Performance, and Space

Sound is not a coat of color painted over abstract notes.

Different ways of producing sound alter how listeners organize sound into
events, streams, layers, and spaces.

### C5.1｜Instruments as Bodies

* **C5.1.01｜An Instrument Has a Body**: An instrument is not an interchangeable timbre label; range, attack, sustain, resonance, inertia, and manner of performance belong to the material of the work.
* **C5.1.02｜A Different Register Is a Different Body**: The same instrument may possess entirely different personae and perceptual positions in different registers.
* **C5.1.03｜Technique Has a Source**: Ornamentation, articulation, vibrato, glissando, excitation, and resonance should begin with how that sound itself comes into being.
* **C5.1.04｜Transcendence Can Be Beautiful**: A virtual instrument may exceed the bounds of human performance, but that transcendence should be treated as an explicit aesthetic rather than disguised as realistic performance.
* **C5.1.05｜Decay Remains Present**: Note-off does not mean that an audible event dies immediately; tails, resonance, and encounters with other parts belong to musical time.

### C5.2｜Timbre and Texture

* **C5.2.01｜Timbre May Legislate**: Changes of timbre may carry theme, boundary, tension, development, and memory rather than merely making the sound attractive.
* **C5.2.02｜Fusion Is Legitimate**: When several sounds fuse into a new composite color, the listener need not be forced to distinguish every source.
* **C5.2.03｜Separation into Streams Is Legitimate**: When independent streams are needed, register, timbre, rhythm, space, or behavior should give them perceptible differences.
* **C5.2.04｜Texture May Hold Sovereignty**: Surface texture itself may become the principal event taking place in the music.
* **C5.2.05｜Change Need Not Be Obvious**: A change of timbre sufficient to alter overall perception need not be immediately verbalizable by the listener.

### C5.3｜Performance

* **C5.3.01｜Performance Belongs to the Work**: Duration, dynamics, articulation, and relationships among gestures are not decorations applied after rendering.
* **C5.3.02｜Expression Has Direction**: Significant microtiming, dynamics, and articulation should be allowed to form syntactic direction across notes.
* **C5.3.03｜Living Microvariation**: Real or credible performance also permits many small differences that need not each bear semantic responsibility.
* **C5.3.04｜Microvariation Is Not White Noise**: Living differences should be constrained by the same performing body, instrumental mechanism, metrical relation, and local state rather than randomizing every parameter independently.
* **C5.3.05｜Repetition Need Not Duplicate the Body**: A phrase's second appearance may preserve structural identity while using slightly different touch, breath, bow speed, or onset.
* **C5.3.06｜The Mechanical Can Also Be Alive**: Completely precise, mechanical, or superhuman performance is equally legitimate when it is the sonic state the work genuinely needs.

### C5.4｜Space

* **C5.4.01｜Space Is Relationship**: Distance, width, dryness, wetness, motion, and reflection may alter role and structure.
* **C5.4.02｜Space Has a History**: The same sound may later become nearer, farther, emptier, or more enclosed to show that the world has changed.
* **C5.4.03｜Reverb Is Not Glue**: Spatial effects must not become a single filter applied to make everything sound “more sophisticated.”
* **C5.4.04｜Invisible Layers Also Occupy Space**: Tails, air, noise, and space itself occupy attention and time and should be heard like any other part.

# C6｜Time, Memory, and Structure

Structure need not be a blueprint known in full before the first note appears.

It may also be a shape gradually revealed as materials interact through time.

### C6.1｜Growing Structure

* **C6.1.01｜Structure May Come Before or After**: One may design structure before writing material, or discover structure after material has appeared.
* **C6.1.02｜The Map Is Not the Territory**: A formal map describes how a work currently holds together; drawing the map must not prohibit a better path from appearing.
* **C6.1.03｜Major Events Have Histories**: The more authority an event bears as climax, rupture, return, or change of identity, the more it requires preparation, memory, or consequences.
* **C6.1.04｜Small Events May Have No Consequences**: Not every sound must be recovered in the future.
* **C6.1.05｜Repetition Establishes a World**: Repetition does more than economize material; it teaches the listener what is normal.
* **C6.1.06｜Change Alters the Past**: The return of the same material in a new context may alter how the listener understands its first appearance.
* **C6.1.07｜Return Carries History**: A return may be faithful, incomplete, transformed, distant, or alienated, but it should know that it has happened before.
* **C6.1.08｜A Climax Is Not Obligatory**: Not every work needs a single highest point; drift, cycles, fading, juxtaposition, and structures without climax are all legitimate.
* **C6.1.09｜Boundaries May Be Hard or Soft**: Sections may be divided clearly or may change like a climate.
* **C6.1.10｜Silence Occupies Time**: After sound disappears, the listener's expectation, memory, and sense of resonance continue.
* **C6.1.11｜Distant Causes Are Legitimate**: An early event that seemed small may reveal its meaning only much later.
* **C6.1.12｜The Ending May Answer in Many Ways**: A work may complete, rewrite, suspend, dissolve, cycle, refuse, or stop abruptly; what matters is that its ending belongs to the world that preceded it.

# C7｜Manyan, Style, and Culture

Within an established world, a local necessity may arise in which nothing else
will do.

Yet another complete world may grow from the same starting point and develop a
different, equally rigorous necessity.

These two facts do not conflict.

## C7.1｜Many Worlds

* **C7.1.01｜Necessity Is Not Unique**: The fact that A cannot be otherwise in World A does not make World B wrong.
* **C7.1.02｜A Branch Must Stand on Its Own**: If a difference continues to alter what follows, it should be treated as a complete world of the work rather than a local skin that can be plugged in or removed at will.
* **C7.1.03｜Common Origins Need No Winner**: Several branches may share an identity kernel, originating material, or early history while holding different stances.
* **C7.1.04｜Incomparability Is Legitimate**: When two candidates protect distinct and incommensurable values, they must not be forced into a single overall ranking.
* **C7.1.05｜Do Not Average Worlds**: Do not combine two branches merely to retain the strengths of both if the compromise causes each to lose its edge.
* **C7.1.06｜Publication Is Not a Verdict**: Choosing one version for public release does not prove the other complete branches wrong.
* **C7.1.07｜A Side Branch May Sleep**: A branch not currently being developed may preserve its identity and history and re-enter creation in the future.
* **C7.1.08｜Qiyun May Also Branch**: Even with similar underlying form, two worlds may become genuinely different works through different sonic ecologies, spaces, performances, and relationships among small sounds.

## C7.2｜Style and Culture

* **C7.2.01｜The Specific Comes Before the General Category**: When discussing culture or tradition, a specific region, period, use, and performance practice take precedence over broad labels such as “Chinese style,” “classical,” or “folk.”
* **C7.2.02｜Against Tokenization**: A scale, an ethnic instrument, a rhythmic pattern, or a timbre cannot by itself represent an entire culture.
* **C7.2.03｜Relationships Matter More Than Checklists**: Style depends more on how material is organized, performed, breathed, repeated, and ended than on the number of emblematic elements present.
* **C7.2.04｜Heterogeneity May Be Preserved**: Cross-cultural or cross-style fusion need not smooth away differences; friction itself may become part of the work.
* **C7.2.05｜Unfamiliarity Is Not a Defect**: A musical language must not be presumed to need correction merely because it differs from mainstream training.
* **C7.2.06｜The Name Does Not Bind the Style**: The cultural origin of the name “Tianlai” does not require users to create Chinese-style music; it governs how differences are regarded, not what cultural surface the sound must assume.

# C8｜Listening Review, Agents, and Evolution

The Constitution's final task is not to tell creators “what should be changed.”

It is to ensure that a work already capable of making sense on its own still has
an opportunity to expose problems that language and reasoning have not found.

## C8.1｜Five Ways of Listening

Each significant final review should distinguish at least the following five
ways of listening. They need not produce numerical scores.

### 1. Listen for the Underlying Form

Temporarily set aside lavish timbre and detail, and ask:

* Where does the work's identity reside?
* Why does time continue?
* Do repetition and change genuinely form a history?
* If the principal material itself does not hold, is rendering concealing that fact?

### 2. Listen to the Whole World

Do not separate the parts or analyze theory; listen only to the complete result:

* Has it formed a complete sonic world?
* After three minutes, what remains: a melody, or an entire experience?
* Where is something correct but lifeless?

### 3. Listen from the Periphery

Deliberately move attention away from the principal voice:

* What are the other lives doing while the principal melody continues?
* When the principal melody rests, does the world immediately collapse into a chordal background?
* Are there small lines worth following that never force the listener to follow them?
* Is the background alive, or merely performing its “accompaniment duty”?

### 4. Listen by Subtraction

Mute or delete a questionable small sound, background, texture, or spatial
element for an A/B comparison:

* What truly dies when it is removed?
* Even if nothing can be put into words, does the ear reliably find the result thinner, harder, less credible, more static, or more directionless?
* If the difference cannot be reproduced reliably, does it deserve to keep occupying complexity?

### 5. Listen for History

When hearing repetition and return:

* Does the world remember the first appearance when the material returns?
* Is the change merely a parameter change, or has time genuinely passed?
* Do all the parts forget what they have experienced, as though copied and pasted?

## C8.2｜Separate the Evaluations

* **C8.2.01｜Evaluate Melody Separately**: Whether the melody is good should be reviewed on its own; lavish arrangement must not answer in its place.
* **C8.2.02｜Evaluate the Music Separately**: Whether the entire piece is good must also be reviewed independently; “the principal melody is good” is not an exemption from review.
* **C8.2.03｜Give Qiyun Its Own Dimension**: Chengjing, peripheral life, texture, space, and companion parts should have an independent dimension of observation.
* **C8.2.04｜A Local Part Does Not Bind the Whole**: A part that sounds poor when soloed does not prove that it lacks value in the whole.
* **C8.2.05｜The Whole Does Not Pardon Local Errors**: A convincing overall atmosphere cannot conceal a genuine technical error or structural failure that damages the work.
* **C8.2.06｜Difference Before Value**: If listeners cannot reliably perceive a difference between two versions, do not rush to construct an aesthetic theory for that difference.
* **C8.2.07｜Reasons Come Afterward**: Record what changed in the listening experience before explaining why; do not begin with a theory and force the ear to find evidence for it.
* **C8.2.08｜Irreplaceability Can Be Plural**: Irreplaceability does not mean “there is only one correct version”; it means that replacing something within this world would lose an important relationship.

## C8.3｜Agents

* **C8.3.01｜Clauses Generate Questions**: An Agent should use the Constitution to ask questions, not use clauses to generate answers directly.
* **C8.3.02｜A Clause Is Not a Reason**: “Because Cx.xx requires it” is not a sufficient reason for a musical choice.
* **C8.3.03｜Distinguish Four Things**: An Agent should distinguish facts, hypotheses, explanations, and judgments, never presenting one as another.
* **C8.3.04｜Permit Seeds**: During generation, an Agent may propose material without a complete reason; later listening and Xiangyin determine whether it enters the work's lasting history.
* **C8.3.05｜Do Not Manufacture Conflict**: When there is no real doubt, do not invent multiple plans and false conflicts merely to complete a process.
* **C8.3.06｜Do Not Manufacture Necessity**: If several directions could work, acknowledge uncertainty or enter a branch rather than forcibly proving one to be “uniquely correct.”
* **C8.3.07｜Do Not Feed on Preference Libraries**: Historical works, award results, user preferences, and past successes may be studied and consulted, but they must not secretly become factual premises in the current work's composition map.
* **C8.3.08｜Do Not Let the Majority Crush the Heterogeneous**: A musical choice does not gain greater legitimacy merely because it is more common in training data.
* **C8.3.09｜The More Fluent the Explanation, the More Caution Is Due**: When an Agent can explain a choice with great fluency, it should check especially carefully whether that explanation points to musical facts.
* **C8.3.10｜Do Not Outsource Aesthetic Judgment**: An Agent may propose, derive, challenge, branch, and review; whether a version is ultimately worth keeping remains a matter for listening.

## C8.4｜Completion

* **C8.4.01｜Completion Does Not Mean No Open Problems**: A work need not eliminate every open question before it can be complete.
* **C8.4.02｜Completion Does Not Mean No Further Change Is Possible**: Every work can still be altered; “it can still be changed” is not a sufficient reason to continue revising.
* **C8.4.03｜Completion Depends on Gain**: When further changes can no longer reliably increase the value this world seeks to protect, continued iteration should be questioned.
* **C8.4.04｜Preserve the Edge**: In the final stages, be especially wary of erasing a work's distinctiveness merely to smooth out local irregularities.
* **C8.4.05｜A Mystery May Remain**: If something genuinely works yet still cannot be fully explained, let the explanation owe a debt to the work rather than making the work owe a debt to the explanation.

# Appendix A｜Proposed Chengjing Stage for the Workflow

This stage comes after the principal structure, Xiangyin derivations, composition
map, and whole-work review, and before finalization or branching.

It is not a “decoration stage,” nor does it add instruments automatically.

Proposed name:

**Chengjing / Qiyun Pass**

Its input must be a complete candidate that can already stand on its own.

Fixed questions:

1. When the principal melody temporarily withdraws, what continues in the world?
2. Are all non-principal parts merely chordal filling, metrical repetition, or copies of the principal melody?
3. Does the peripheral world of a repeated section carry a small history of time having passed?
4. Are there at least some sonic relationships that operate only in peripheral perception?
5. Are timbre, space, resonance, and performance merely static presets, or do they grow with the work?
6. Has fear of emptiness filled places that should have been allowed to breathe?
7. Are newly added “refined details” merely predictable decorative formulas?
8. After a deletion A/B of a candidate small sound, does the whole suffer a stable and valuable loss?

### Revision Authority During Chengjing

By default, only limited and reversible microscopic revisions are allowed:

- add or remove companion parts;
- revise a small number of inner voices, responses, and short gestures;
- adjust local timbre, dynamics, duration, and articulation;
- adjust entrances, exits, empty space, and resonance;
- adjust local spatial relationships;
- revise the peripheral ecology of repeated sections without changing the identity kernel.

If a revision begins to alter:

- the identity kernel;
- principal harmonic causality;
- the fate of a theme;
- sectional function;
- the basis of a climax;
- the ending's response;
- a claim in the originating charter;

then it must immediately leave Chengjing and return to the preceding layer through
the formal `revision_scope`, where the existing Workflow must rebuild the
composition map, derivations, and whole-work review.

### Outcomes Permitted by Chengjing

It may produce:

**A. No revision needed**<br>
The work's exposure, sparseness, or stillness already holds.

**B. Microscopic improvement**<br>
The world is more alive, but its underlying form and causal relationships remain
unchanged.

**C. Structural problem discovered**<br>
Refinement has exposed a deeper problem; return to formal revision.

**D. Another world discovered**<br>
The same underlying form has become a complete existence that the original
version cannot absorb because of a different ecology of parts, orchestration, or
spatial relationship; enter a whole-work fork.

# Appendix B｜Principles for Migrating Content from v0.1

The following content is not without value; it is simply no longer suitable for
the highest constitutional level:

**Specific practices of melody, harmony, rhythm, and orchestration**<br>
→ move to the *Composition and Orchestration Reference*

**Style recipes, emotional types, and cases in cultural vocabulary**<br>
→ move to style references and case libraries

**Instrumental ranges, techniques, sound sources, and performance constraints**<br>
→ move to the instrument roster and instrument-specific materials

**Spectrum, loudness, dynamics, mastering, and technical quality control**<br>
→ move to the production manual and machine checks

**Fields of the work charter, Xiangyin, the composition map, revision contracts,
evidence, and branching procedures**<br>
→ fall under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Creative Workflow

**The Constitution retains only what these systems cannot replace:**

> What has the right to exist first.<br>
> What begins to carry consequences once it has been established.<br>
> What has the right to live even when it is not at center stage.<br>
> What may become another complete world.<br>
> And when we should stop explaining and listen again.

# Discipline of Constitutional Revision

* A rule that can be precisely replaced by a technical check should be removed from the Constitution.
* A rule that repeatedly holds only within one kind of style should be demoted to stylistic law or precedent.
* A rule that forces the generative stage to explain music that has not yet formed should receive priority scrutiny.
* Every new restriction must state both what it protects and what it might kill.
* Every constitutional revision must preserve earlier versions; earlier works are not retroactively judged wrong because a new Constitution exists.
* The maturity of the Constitution is measured not by an ever-growing number of clauses, but by fewer clauses protecting an ever-larger creative space.
